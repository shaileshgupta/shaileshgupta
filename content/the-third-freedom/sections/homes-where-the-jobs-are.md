## The idea

Picture a young nurse in Mumbai who rents a flat ten minutes from a metro station. The contract is written, the deposit is two months' rent, and both she and her landlord know any dispute is settled in 60 days.

Picture an architect in Pune who files a plan online on Monday. Software checks it, a licensed engineer certifies it, and by the next week the family is building.

I believe a city is its people. For years we held our cities low with tight limits on height, and kept old rent laws that made letting a flat feel risky. Lifting those limits is the fastest way I know to more homes. When land near transport can rise, when renting is safe for both sides, and when a certified plan becomes a permit in days, you get good homes, short commutes and calmer, safer streets.

We already have the tools: metro lines, permit software, noise sensors and the playbook that built Aadhaar and UPI. This chapter simply puts them to work together.

## Why now

Here is why I think the moment is now.

Homes are already waiting. About **1.1 crore** urban homes stood vacant at the 2011 Census, and safe renting can bring many of them to families who need them.

Our station land can carry far more. Plots near most metro stations may build only about **1.5** times their area today. Letting them rise to 5 times puts new homes where the trains already run.

And the law is ready. The Model Tenancy Act of 2021 has been adopted by **4** states, and one central Act brings its benefits to every city.

## The big moves

Here is the path I see, one step after another.

1.  **First, make renting safe for owners and tenants.** We pass one central Tenancy Act in the first session of Parliament, built on the Model Tenancy Act: rent by written contract, a modest deposit, and a Rent Court that settles disputes within 60 days. Old controlled tenancies, such as Mumbai's pagdi system, move to market rent in gentle steps, and the tenant receives a flat of the same area when the building is redeveloped. Owners gain the confidence to rent, and vacant homes fill.
    
2.  **Next, let homes rise near metro stations, with pipes and roads first.** For example, plots close to a metro or rapid-rail station could build up to 5 times their area once the water utility confirms capacity. The extra property tax from higher values stays with the city and pays for water, sewers and roads ahead of the homes.
    
3.  **At the same time, grant building permits in days.** A plan filed online, checked by software and certified by a licensed engineer becomes the permit, with random audits keeping quality high. One way is a 7-day clock, with the permit granted on day 8 unless an objection is raised.
    
4.  **Then, make every city safe and quiet to live in.** Every certifying engineer stands behind the building for 10 years, and tall buildings carry a fire certificate from a fire engineer assigned at random. Every city of 1 lakh people or more publishes a noise map and a plan to quieten its loudest roads, rail lines and airports, on the European model, so the homes we build are also good places to live. Clean air has its own chapter ([Chapter 21](/the-third-freedom/clean-air-for-every-lung)).
    
5.  **Finally, leave no bare ground.** Walk past almost any new building in India and you see the problem: a strip of loose earth between the gate and the road that turns to dust in summer, to mud and stagnant pools in the monsoon, and to a dumping spot in between. So every developer finishes every open patch inside the plot and the public strip in front of it, out to the road edge, with paving, grass or planting and a drain connected to the city's network, as Singapore requires. While building, exposed earth is covered within 14 days of work stopping, as America's construction rules require. And once the building is done, the owner keeps that strip clean and whole, as every New York property owner does for the pavement outside. The completion certificate is issued only when the strip is finished.
    

## Today vs the new way

Here is how I see the new way comparing with today on homes, renting, permits and city life.

| Outcome | Today | The new way |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Time to get a building permit | Months | 7 days; granted on day 8 if no objection |
| Homes near metro stations | Low floor-space limits, about 1.5 times plot area | Up to 5 times plot area; about 77 lakh new homes after year 1 (estimate) |
| Vacant homes put to use | About 1.1 crore vacant (2011) | About 33 lakh new rentals, at no cost to the state (estimate) |
| Settling a rent dispute | Slow, uncertain | Rent Court decides within 60 days |
| Checks on tall buildings | Varies by city | A certifying engineer with 10-year liability, a randomly assigned fire engineer, and 20% audited every year |
| Open ground around new buildings | Bare earth left to the city: dust in summer, mud and stagnant water in the monsoon | Every open patch in the plot and the strip out to the road edge paved or planted, with a working drain, before the completion certificate |
| Noise from roads, rail and airports | Limits set by the Noise Rules of 2000 | A public noise map and action plan every 5 years, with 30% fewer people affected by transport noise by year 5 |

On every count families and cities gain, and the new homes come from private building and private renting, with the pipes and roads paid by each zone's own rising property tax.

## Proof it works

**Auckland built more homes by letting land rise.** After the city upzoned about three-quarters of its residential land in 2016, building consents for new homes rose sharply in the upzoned areas.

**Singapore and New York leave no bare ground.** In Singapore, a developer builds the footpath, drain and green verge along the road in front of its site to the transport authority's standard and hands them over, and a site that lets water stand and mosquitoes breed is fined and can be stopped. In New York, the owner of every building is responsible by law for the pavement outside it, and America's national construction permit requires exposed soil to be covered within 14 days of work stopping.

**Europe maps its noise in public.** Under the Environmental Noise Directive of 2002, every large city and every major road, railway and airport in the European Union publishes a strategic noise map and an action plan every 5 years, after public consultation. Europe now aims for 30% fewer people affected by transport noise, and India can take the same path.

## What success looks like

-   **In the first 30 days:** the central Tenancy Bill is tabled, and the model permit-and-FSI law goes to states with its grant terms.
-   **Within 1 year:** the Tenancy Act is in force across India, and 7-day permits are live in every adopting state.
-   **Within 1 year:** taller-building zones are notified around operational metro stations in adopting states, and every city of 1 lakh people or more publishes its first noise map and action plan.
-   **Within 1 year:** every new completion certificate requires a finished frontage strip, and every active building site covers bare earth within 14 days of work stopping.
-   **After year 1:** about 77 lakh new homes rise near stations (estimate), about 33 lakh vacant homes come onto the rental market (estimate), and every existing plot's frontage is finished by its owner.

## Guardrail ideas

*Ideas to start the conversation, for the builders, experts and the public to shape together.*

-   **Buildings are safe by design.** Every certifying engineer carries 10-year liability, and tall buildings carry a fire certificate from a randomly assigned fire engineer.
-   **Quiet streets, measured in public.** Every city of 1 lakh people or more publishes a noise map and a plan to quieten its loudest roads, rail lines and airports every 5 years, on the model of Europe's Environmental Noise Directive, and aims for Europe's goal of 30% fewer people affected by transport noise.
-   **No bare ground, checked from the sky.** A strip left bare, or water standing on a site for more than 48 hours, earns a fine per square metre for every month it stays that way. After 30 days' notice, the city finishes the work itself and adds the cost to the owner's property tax account. Drones and geotagged photos check every completion, and 5% of sites are audited at random.
-   **Families in need are covered.** Any tenant household under the [Chapter 23](/the-third-freedom/a-hand-up-and-an-equal-chance) income test receives the standard transfer within 30 days, like every other household.
-   **Rents are visible to all.** Rents are published every quarter for every city of 10 lakh people or more, so tenants and owners agree on fair terms.

## FAQs

**Q:** What happens to long-term tenants when rent control ends?

**A:** They gain a home of their own. The move to market rent comes in 4 gentle steps, the tenant receives a flat of the same area when the building is redeveloped, and any household under the income test receives the [Chapter 23](/the-third-freedom/a-hand-up-and-an-equal-chance) transfer. Meanwhile, new supply from taller buildings and vacant homes keeps rents in reach, and rents are published every quarter in every large city.

**Q:** Can water, sewers and roads keep up with taller buildings?

**A:** Yes, because infrastructure comes first. A zone opens to taller buildings only once the water utility certifies capacity, and bonds backed by the zone's extra property tax build pipes and roads ahead of the homes.

**Q:** Is it fair to ask an owner to finish land outside the plot?

**A:** I think it is the fairest rule of all, because the owner gains most from a clean, dry frontage, and the city gains a finished street at no cost to the taxpayer. The strip is small: from the boundary to the road edge, never more than 5 metres. It stays public land, open to everyone, and dig-once ducts run beneath it ([Chapter 20](/the-third-freedom/built-right-built-to-last)).

**Q:** Are 7-day permits safe?

**A:** They are faster and accountable. Each plan carries the signature of a licensed engineer with 10-year liability, 5% of permits are audited at random, and tall buildings get a randomly assigned fire engineer with 20% audited every year.
