## The idea

Picture a young founder in Bengaluru deciding where to build her company. She likes India's three-tax system and its open land market. Her one question is simple: will it last beyond the next election? When she learns that these reforms are written into the Constitution itself, she signs the lease.

I believe reforms written only in ordinary law are a promise for one term, while reforms written into the Constitution become a promise for a generation. Many ideas in this book touch the Constitution: the tax lists, police, land, reservations, pardons, civil-service tenure and local bodies. Placing them there gives citizens, investors and states one lasting answer: this is how India now works.

## Why now

We have done this fast before. The 103rd Amendment passed both Houses in **2 days** in 2019.

The numbers are within reach. Changes to federal subjects need ratification by **14** of the 28 states, and the governing alliance runs 21 of them. Two-thirds in both Houses needs about **70** more Lok Sabha votes, a coalition that GST showed can be built when states and voters gain.

Getting procedure right is the whole game. In **2021** the Supreme Court struck down most of the 97th Amendment because it changed state subjects without ratification. One clear map keeps every reform safe.

## The big moves

1.  **First, draw one map of every change.** Each reform is matched to its Article, its procedure (ordinary law, special majority, or special majority plus state ratification) and its legal risk. The full table is in the Annex.
    
2.  **Next, three bills on day 1 of a special session.** Bill A carries the special-majority changes, such as pardons and civil-service contracts. Bill B carries the changes that need the states, such as the tax lists, police, land and local bodies. Act C carries the ordinary laws. Each is voted schedule by schedule, so every item stands on its own merits.
    
3.  **Meanwhile, build bridges.** Articles 249 and 252 let Parliament legislate for willing states from month 1, so land, police standards and stamp-duty reform move ahead while ratification runs.
    
4.  **Then, ratify within 60 days, with states held whole.** Bill B goes to every legislature the day it passes, aiming for 15 ratifications by day 60. States receive all of the property tax, half of GST and a written share of the bank transaction tax, backed by a monthly guarantee in the first year.
    
5.  **Throughout, choose the strongest form the courts will uphold.** An early reference to the Supreme Court tests the riskiest items, and every clause leaves judicial review, fundamental rights and free and fair elections fully intact.
    

## Today vs the new way

Here is how I see one package comparing with amending piece by piece.

| Outcome | Today | The new way |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Time to pass | Amendments taken one at a time over years | One package passed in 30 days |
| State ratification | About a month for GST | 15 states by day 60, every state voting within 1 year |
| Procedural risk | The 97th Amendment largely struck down in 2021 | Every change mapped to its correct procedure |
| One disputed item | Can hold up a whole bill | Voted schedule by schedule, so the rest proceeds |
| State revenue | Stamp duty and many small taxes | All of property tax, half of GST and a BTT share, with a monthly guarantee |
| Cost | Regular sessions | About ₹135 crore for a 15-day special session (estimate) |

A short session buys every reform a lasting home.

## Proof it works

**GST showed the path.** In 2016 the GST amendment went from Parliament's vote to the President's signature in about a month, with Assam ratifying first. **Subjects have moved before.** The 42nd Amendment (1976) moved education and forests to the Concurrent List, and that move still stands.

## What success looks like

-   **In the first 30 days:** all three bills are published with a plain-English note per clause, passed, and sent to the states.
-   **Within 1 year:** 15 or more ratifications by day 60, and every legislature voting by month 12.
-   **Within 1 year:** a public tracker shows every clause, every named vote and every state's status.

## Guardrail ideas

*Ideas to start the conversation, for the builders, experts and the public to shape together.*

-   **The Constitution's core stays strong.** The Law Ministry certifies in writing that each clause leaves judicial review, Articles 14, 19 and 21 and free and fair elections fully intact.
-   **Every vote is open.** Each clause is published 14 days before its vote, with named votes published within 24 hours.
-   **States are paid on time.** The revenue guarantee is paid by the 10th of each month.

## FAQs

**Q:** Does moving police and land to the Concurrent List weaken federalism?

**A:** I understand the worry, and states keep their laws and their command. Concurrent means shared: state laws stay valid until a central law covers the same ground.

**Q:** Why would states give up stamp duty and their own taxes?

**A:** Because they gain a larger, growing revenue base of their own: all of the property tax, half of GST and a written share of the bank transaction tax, with a monthly guarantee in year 1.

**Q:** Is 30 days enough for scrutiny?

**A:** It is a fair question. Scrutiny is built into every step: 14 days' publication, a Joint Parliamentary Committee report, an early Supreme Court reference and votes schedule by schedule.
