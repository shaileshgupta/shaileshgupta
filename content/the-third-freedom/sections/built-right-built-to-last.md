## The idea

Picture a family in a hill village of 300 people. This year an all-weather road reaches their door. The school bus comes every morning, the ambulance arrives in minutes, and the farmer's tomatoes reach the market fresh.

Picture Ramesh, an auto driver in Indore, on the road outside his house. Water, sewer, power, gas and fibre all go in together, in one trench, in one season, along a street the city has sized for the next 100 years. Then the road rests, smooth, for five full years, and when a neighbour wants broadband the cable is pulled through a duct that already runs to the plot.

Picture a commuter crossing a river bridge. A small sensor on the girder reports its health every day, and a public portal shows the lab test that cleared every stage of its building. She crosses without a second thought.

Picture a young civil engineer who starts her own firm. She passes the construction test, posts a bond, and claims a road package at the same published price as the biggest builder in the country. Her first road is tested, paid within 30 days, and earns her the record that wins the next one.

I believe something simple. We plan once, dig once, build right and guarantee the work. Whoever builds a public work owns its quality, upfront and for years after. When the price is fair and fixed, builders compete on quality and speed. When every test is public, honest builders shine. When the builder stands behind the work for ten years, the work lasts.

We already have the money and the will. What I propose is that we turn every rupee into something safe, finished on time and built to last.

## Why now

The money is already here. Counting support to states, we are spending a record **₹17.1 lakh crore** on public capital works in 2026-27. Quality and speed are now the prize.

Better building frees a fortune. Our large central projects were expected to cost **20.7%** more than planned. If we halve that overrun, a great deal of money goes back to work.

The last mile is within reach. We have already electrified **99.2%** of broad-gauge rail, so we know a national target, tracked in public, gets met.

Digging once saves money every day. Telecom fibre alone is cut about **10 lakh times a year**, and halving those cuts saves about **₹1,500 crore a year** (estimate). About **65%** of our urban settlements have yet to adopt a master plan, so most towns can plan for the century ahead from the start.

Safety is the biggest prize of all. About **1.73 lakh** people lost their lives on our roads in 2023, and the road ministry links many of those deaths to faulty engineering. Sound building, independent testing and live bridge sensors can bring that number steadily down.

## The big moves

Here is the path I see, one step at a time.

1.  **First, publish the last mile and the price for it.** In the first 30 days we publish every road, line, connection and bridge still needed, district by district, each with one fair national price per unit of finished work. One way to set the price is the median of three independent cost consultancies, building on the rate schedules our engineers already use. Published prices replace lowest-bid tenders, so builders compete on quality and speed.
    
2.  **Next, let any certified builder step forward with a bond.** A builder who passes the construction test claims a package by posting a bond upfront, for example 10% of its value. Startups and small firms can enter every field, and each firm grows as its record grows.
    
3.  **Then, test before we pay, and ask the builder to stand behind the work.** An accredited lab, picked at random, tests each stage, the result goes online within days, and a pass releases payment within 30 days. The builder guarantees the structure for ten years, backed by insurance, as France does. Designs meet the Eurocodes, Europe's common standards for structural design, as the reference level, with any gap in India's own codes published every year.
    
4.  **Within the year, finish the last mile.** All broad-gauge rail electrified, every household metered, longer power hours published feeder by feeder, every village road under way, and every old highway bridge inspected, with sensors on those that need watching.
    
5.  **Alongside, give every city one plan for 100 years and one map of everything below.** Every utility puts its network on one shared map, linked to Call Before u Dig, within 30 days, and a radar survey of the main urban roads fills the gaps within a year. Each of our roughly 600 cities of 1 lakh people or more adopts one master plan, drawn by certified planning firms to a national standard, open for public comment and approved by the city CEO ([Chapter 14](/the-third-freedom/leaders-who-run-their-areas-like-ceos)). Each plan starts with a 100-year growth frame: land for future roads, metro and rail lines, utility corridors, parks and water bodies is marked now and kept free, and trunk sewers, water mains and duct banks are laid big enough for that future city, because the digging is most of the cost. Buildings and services then arrive in stages, on a skeleton that never needs redrawing.
    
6.  **Then dig each street once, and let it rest.** Every planned dig is booked ahead on one city portal, so every utility shares the same open trench. Every new or rebuilt road gets shared ducts, and tunnels on the widest roads, with side ducts to every plot; the city owns them and rents space to any certified operator, so a new connection is simply pulled through. A stretch is resurfaced once its utility works are done, the builder's 10-year warranty covers the trench too, and the road rests, smooth, for 5 years. Emergencies are still fixed at once and restored to full quality.
    
7.  **Finally, make accountability clear and fair.** Two short laws, one for public works and one for professional engineers, let a fair hearing bar the rare builder whose shoddy work causes a failure, and protect every engineer who flags a defect. City redevelopment moves by resident choice, with consent recorded on the land registry ([Chapter 5](/the-third-freedom/land-you-truly-own)) and every family rehoused close by in at least the same space.
    

## Today vs the new way

Here is how building under the new rules compares with today on cost, speed and safety.

| Outcome | Today | The new way |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cost overrun on large central projects | 20.7% on average, ₹5.71 lakh crore across 1,817 projects | About half, freeing about ₹2.9 lakh crore over a 5-year build cycle (estimate) |
| How the price is set | Lowest-bid tender, project by project | One published national price per unit of finished work |
| Builder's money at stake | Performance security of 3% to 10% | Bond of 10% upfront, 20% for packages of ₹100 crore or more |
| Quality check before payment | Varies by project | Random accredited lab test at every milestone, public within 72 hours |
| Builder stands behind the work | Varies by contract | 10-year structural warranty, backed by insurance |
| Extra cost of protection | Nil | About 1.5% of value for bonds and insurance, plus 0.3% for testing (estimate), against a 20.7% average overrun |
| Rail electrification | 99.2% of broad gauge | 100% within 1 year |
| Map of what lies under a street | Separate drawings for each utility | One shared map of every line, complete within 1 year |
| Life of a resurfaced road | Often cut again within months | Whole for 5 years |
| Fibre cuts and their cost | About 10 lakh cuts a year, about ₹3,000 crore lost | Cuts halved, about ₹1,500 crore a year saved (estimate) |
| Planning horizon | 20-year plans, often outgrown within a decade | A 100-year growth frame with corridors kept free now, built in stages |
| City income from ducts | Nil | About ₹2,400 crore a year once built out (estimate) |

For a small share of project value, India gets cities planned for a century, streets dug once, and works that are finished closer to budget, tested in public and guaranteed for a decade.

## Proof it works

**America has bonded its builders since 1935.** The Miller Act requires every contractor on a federal construction contract above $100,000 to post a performance bond and a payment bond before the work is awarded. Builders carry the risk of their own work, and projects get finished.

**France guarantees every building for ten years.** Its compulsory "décennale" insurance makes the builder responsible for structural defects for a decade, so owners are covered and builders build carefully.

**India already prices work from published schedules.** CPWD, MoRTH and the Jal Jeevan Mission price work from published schedules of rates. The national rate card builds on what Indian engineers already use every day.

**India can hit a national last-mile target.** Broad-gauge rail electrification reached 99.2% by November 2025, tracked in public, step by step.

**Long plans outlast their makers.** In 1811, New York laid out a street grid for an island that was mostly farmland; two centuries later Manhattan still runs on it. In the 1860s Joseph Bazalgette built London's sewers and, by most accounts, doubled the pipe sizes his numbers called for; the city has grown several times over and they still carry its waste. Singapore's Concept Plan looks 40 to 50 years ahead and keeps land free for metro lines decades before they are built.

**Gujarat already digs once.** GIFT City runs a utility tunnel about 16 km long. Power, water, district cooling, data cables and automated waste pipes all share it, and the streets above stay clear.

**Indian builders already deliver at speed and on budget.** Reliance built the Jamnagar refinery in 33 months, Jio reached 100 million customers in 170 days, and Tata Motors built its Sanand plant in 14 months. One owner per project, a locked budget and date, standard designs and a daily control room are habits Indian industry already lives by, and every public project here adopts them.

## What success looks like

-   **In the first 30 days:** the national rate card and the last-mile gap list are published by district, the upfront bond applies to all new work, and every new milestone payment follows a random lab test.
-   **In the first 30 days:** every bridge flagged "poor" is inspected, with results public, every utility has uploaded its maps, the dig booking portal is live, and the Model Dig-Once Act is offered to every state.
-   **Within 1 year:** 100% of broad-gauge rail is electrified, every household is metered, and urban and rural feeders reach their published hours.
-   **Within 1 year:** every habitation road package is claimed and under way, every national highway bridge older than 25 years is inspected, and latent-defect insurance covers all public structures.
-   **Within 1 year:** every city of 1 lakh or more has adopted its master plan with a published 100-year growth frame and corridor map, the radar survey of every main urban road is complete, every new or rebuilt urban road is built with ducts, and the first 50 utility tunnels are under construction.
-   **After year 1:** new national corridors are built at the rate card, every package bonded, tested and warrantied, with cost per km published on the scorecard ([Chapter 14](/the-third-freedom/leaders-who-run-their-areas-like-ceos)); utility tunnels serve the 500 busiest kilometres, and duct rent brings cities about **₹2,400 crore a year** (estimate).

## Guardrail ideas

*Ideas to start the conversation, for the builders, experts and the public to shape together.*

-   **Builders are paid on time.** Payment arrives within 30 days of a test pass, with the government paying 1.5% interest a month on any delay.
-   **Test results everyone can trust.** A second random lab re-runs 5% of tests, with an audit of both labs whenever results differ by more than 10%.
-   **Fair process before any bar.** Each case gets a hearing within 30 days, a decision within 60 days and an appeal to a tribunal decided within 90 days, with asset recovery only by court order and capped at repair cost, compensation and a 2× penalty.
-   **Every family moves straight into a finished home.** Residents move only once the new home has an occupancy certificate, with the builder paying market rent plus 20% for any late handover.
-   **Every dig is planned and booked.** Any unbooked cut carries a clear fine, a multiple of the restoration cost, so planning becomes the easy path for every utility.
-   **Corridors stay free, owners are paid fairly.** No permanent building goes up on a reserved corridor. An owner can use that land for anything temporary in the meantime, and is paid market value, or given transfer of development rights at once, when the corridor is needed.
-   **Duct space is open to all.** Duct rent is set by independent cost experts, published for each city, and offered first come first served, with the city CEO able to grant space within 7 days.

## FAQs

**Q:** Will a 20% bond keep small builders out?

**A:** Small builders gain a fair, open door to public work. Packages below ₹100 crore need a 10% bond, the bond can be a surety bond costing about 1% a year rather than cash, and certification rests on the test and the bond alone. Each firm grows as its record and bonds grow.

**Q:** Will engineers hesitate to sign off work?

**A:** Honest engineers gain clear protection. The offence covers only unsafe work certified knowingly or with reason to know. Any engineer who flags a defect in writing on the portal is fully protected, and liability insurance is part of every engineer's certification.

**Q:** Can India really cover the whole country in one year?

**A:** India completes the last mile in one year and builds everything after that to the new standard. Rail electrification, household meters, feeder hours and every habitation road under way are the year-1 targets. New national corridors follow under the same rules from day 30.

**Q:** Isn't 100 years too far ahead to plan?

**A:** The plan fixes the part that lasts: the streets, the transit lines, the trunk pipes and the open spaces. Buildings change every generation, and the skeleton lets them. Manhattan's grid has carried two centuries of change for exactly this reason. Each plan is still reviewed every 10 years, and the corridors stay put.

**Q:** Roads and water belong to states and cities. How does dig-once reach them?

**A:** Every state gains from joining, and central powers start the work on day one. A Model Dig-Once Act is offered to every state, linked to 20% of central urban grants, and existing central laws cover telecom, power and gas from the first day.
