## The idea

Picture a farmer in a hill village in Uttarakhand. This year a satellite link gives his village its first mobile signal, he has a made-in-India smartphone he is paying off at ₹200 a month, and he applies for crop insurance in Hindi at the AI Seva terminal in the panchayat office, speaking instead of typing.

Picture a young woman in Kochi opening a brokerage account. She taps "share my KYC" on her phone and the broker gets three answers: verified, over 18, resident. That evening she opens DigiLocker and sees exactly which offices looked at her records this month, and why.

Picture a small software firm in Indore building a better land-records app on an open government API.

I believe India has already discovered something the rest of the world is still learning. With UPI and Aadhaar we showed that open public rails, built by the state and opened to everyone, beat closed systems built by a single vendor. Now every public service can run that way. The state builds the switch and the registry, sets the standard and guarantees uptime. You hold the keys to your own data. And every Indian, in every village, can reach it all.

## Why now

The model is proven at national scale. In October 2026 UPI handled **25.47 billion** payments, about 82 crore a day, on rails any certified bank or app can build on.

The last mile is within reach. Only about **10,100** villages still wait for a mobile signal, a gap a satellite link can close within months.

The savings are real. A KYC check costs up to about **₹1,000** on paper, and every firm repeats it for the same person; a reusable check at ₹1 or less brings that close to zero.

And the core build is modest. About **₹5,000 crore** (estimate) pays for the open gateway, the privacy ledger, the KYC credential and AI Seva terminals in about 2.6 lakh panchayats.

## The big moves

Here is one clear path, step by step. The details belong to the mission heads, industry and the builders who will take it on.

1.  **First, make every portal reliable and visible.** Every citizen-facing portal shows a live status page measured by an independent monitor, with a clear target such as 99.9% uptime a month. The vendor answers first for any shortfall, and the agency head answers for enforcing the contract.
    
2.  **Next, open every service to builders.** Every government service publishes an open API and a test sandbox, like UPI, ONDC and Account Aggregator. The government runs the switch and the registry. Any certified company, including a startup, builds the apps on top, so you get choice and the state gets competition.
    
3.  **Then, put each citizen in charge of their data.** Every access to your data appears in a tamper-proof log you can see in DigiLocker. Personal records are encrypted with keys you hold, with easy recovery at any post office. A firm gets your data only with your consent, for a named purpose. Checks such as "over 18?" return just yes or no, and KYC is done once for life.
    
4.  **Then, bring every village and every person online.** Every village gets a signal, satellite first and towers after. Anyone can get one low-cost, made-in-India smartphone as a repayable loan. Voice-first AI Seva terminals in 22 languages reach every panchayat and post office. Biometric sign-in always comes with a fallback, such as OTP or a nominee, so every benefit reaches its owner.
    
5.  **Finally, let the nation see its money trail and its pulse.** Banks and payment apps share transaction data with the Financial Intelligence Unit in real time, so AI can spot terror financing and laundering as it happens; privacy rules protect messages and personal records while the money trail stays open to monitoring. A National Data Analytics Centre gives every ministry and chief minister a daily dashboard built from anonymised counts.

## Proof it works

**We built the world's open payment rail.** UPI is an open standard on which any certified bank or app can build, and it handled 25.47 billion payments in October 2026.

**Estonia lets citizens see who looked.** Its Data Tracker shows each person which state body accessed their data in national registers, and when.

**Consent works at scale.** India's Account Aggregator system, where people approve each data request on their phone, crossed 100 million consents within three years.

**Real-time data guides policy.** The UK Office for National Statistics publishes weekly real-time indicators of the economy built from card spending and other fast data.

## Guardrail ideas

*Ideas to start the conversation, for the builders, experts and the public to shape together.*

-   **Every person gets their benefit.** If biometric failures in any district pass 1% in a month, that district switches to fallback by default.
-   **Data opens only by law.** Police and agencies reach personal records through a court order, with every access logged and shown to the person, and with dismissal and prosecution for anyone who looks without an open case.
-   **The phone loan stays light.** Repayments stay within 10% of a household's monthly transfer, any balance left after 36 months is written off, and the phone stays with its owner.
-   **Open systems keep every vendor replaceable.** Every terminal and phone runs open standards, so any certified maker can step in within 30 days.

## FAQs

**Q:** If citizens hold their own keys, can police and courts still reach evidence?

**A:** Yes, and you gain real security. A court order names the data, and the citizen or the recovery holders release it; refusing is contempt, as with any evidence. With key recovery on by default, lawful access works for almost everyone, and the money trail stays fully open to FIU monitoring.

**Q:** Is a government-backed phone a hidden subsidy or a surveillance device?

**A:** It is a fair loan that puts every citizen online, and the worry is a reasonable one to raise. Everyone gets the same terms, the only cost is shown openly as a ₹5,000 crore provision, and the phone runs an open system with only DigiLocker and UPI preinstalled.

**Q:** Does real-time payment monitoring end financial privacy?

**A:** It protects honest people while catching terror financing and laundering early. The system reads the money trail banks already report under the PMLA. Messages and personal records still need a judge's warrant, and every look-up is logged and later shown to the person.
