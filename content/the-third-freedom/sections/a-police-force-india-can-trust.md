## The idea

Let me start with a woman in Lucknow walking into a police station to report a theft. The station is clean, the CCTV light is green, and the officer at the desk wears a body camera. Her complaint is registered at once. If she were ever mistreated, an independent authority chaired by a retired judge would decide her complaint within 90 days.

Now picture a young constable in Patna. He earned his place on one open merit exam, passed an army-standard fitness test and spent 9 months at the academy learning drill, law and human rights. His pay matches the market for his skill and risk, and his contract is renewed on published results.

And picture a terror suspect arrested after months of careful, warrant-backed intelligence work. His trial ends within 90 days of a full charge-sheet, with a state-paid senior lawyer at his side and an automatic appeal to the High Court.

I believe safety is the foundation of prosperity. A police force earns trust when it is well staffed, well led, well trained and visibly accountable. Our police can carry the discipline and pride of our army, and the fairness of our courts.

## Why now

The quickest win is simply filling the posts we already have. India has about **120 police per lakh people**, against a global norm of 222. About **6.2 lakh** sanctioned posts, state and central, are waiting to be filled, and filling them is also 6.2 lakh good jobs.

Results can rise sharply. Of more than 6,500 people arrested under the anti-terror law in 2018 to 2022, **252** were convicted. Better investigation and faster trials turn arrests into justice.

Faster trials also free our prisons. Undertrials make up **74%** of all prisoners, and every case decided quickly returns a cell, a courtroom and often a life.

## The big moves

1.  **First, one national standard, with states in command.** We start with a National Police Standards Act: one rulebook for fitness, training, promotion and equipment, which states adopt through a Model Police Act, encouraged by police grants. [Chapter 28](/the-third-freedom/locking-in-the-third-freedom) later makes police a shared Concurrent subject, and states keep command of their forces throughout.
    
2.  **Next, build an army culture through people and training.** One open merit exam for every new constable and sub-inspector, with army-standard fitness and drill. Ex-servicemen and ex-Agniveers compete on the same test, where their training counts, and proven leaders from disciplined services can enter at Deputy SP level. Every recruit spends 9 months at the academy, with law and human rights at the heart of it.
    
3.  **Then, fill every vacancy, on contract and at market pay.** Rolling recruitment fills every sanctioned post while holding the bar high. Each post becomes a renewable contract at market pay, with a premium for frontline risk and a bonus tied to published results ([Chapter 12](/the-third-freedom/a-lean-state-that-delivers)). States fund the growth by selling or closing boards and corporations the market can run.
    
4.  **Make policing visible.** Every frontline officer wears a body camera and station CCTV streams to a secure cloud. An independent Police Complaints Authority in every state and district, led by a retired judge, decides the gravest complaints within 90 days.
    
5.  **Finally, lawful intelligence and fast, fair security trials.** A National Internal Intelligence Act gives the Intelligence Bureau a legal footing, with every search approved by a High Court judge and oversight by a parliamentary committee with an opposition chair. Special courts finish terror and sabotage trials within 90 days of a full charge-sheet, with state-paid counsel and an automatic appeal.
    

## Today vs the new way

Here is how I see a modern police force comparing with today on strength, results and cost.

| Outcome | Today | The new way |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Police strength | About 120 per lakh people; about 6.2 lakh posts vacant | Every sanctioned post filled; at least 40% of new recruits on duty within year 1 |
| How constables are hired | Different rules in each state | One open merit exam with army-standard fitness |
| Security trials | 252 convictions from over 6,500 anti-terror arrests in 2018 to 2022 | Trials finished within 90 days of a full charge-sheet, appeal within 90 days |
| Cameras | Station CCTV patchy; a Supreme Court case on it in 2025 | Every frontline officer on body camera; station footage kept 18 months |
| Complaints | Complaints bodies in some states | An authority in every state and district, grave cases decided in 90 days |
| Cost to states | About ₹1.85 lakh crore a year on police | About ₹70,000 crore a year more (estimate), paid inside today's state wage bill by selling or closing other units: about the same total |
| Cost to the Centre | Police modernisation grants | Equipment paid from existing grants: no new central money |

We get a far stronger, fairer police for about the same total public wage bill.

## Proof it works

**Kerala built independent complaints bodies.** The Kerala Police Act 2011 set up statutory complaints authorities at state and district level, chaired by retired judges.

**Body cameras changed behaviour.** A 2012 randomised trial in Rialto, California, found that body cameras roughly halved police use of force and sharply cut complaints against officers.

**Judges approve surveillance in the United Kingdom.** Under the Investigatory Powers Act 2016, a minister's warrant takes effect once an independent judicial commissioner also approves it, a "double lock".

**Trained soldiers already join India's forces.** In December 2025 the Home Ministry raised the share of BSF constable posts for ex-Agniveers to 50%.

## What success looks like

-   **In the first 30 days:** every station CCTV is working, the open merit exam and fitness test are notified, the body-camera specification is published, and existing NIA and UAPA courts are designated as special courts with the 90-day rule.
-   **Within 1 year:** every vacancy is advertised, at least 80% appointed and at least 40% on duty.
-   **Within 1 year:** every frontline officer wears a body camera, and complaints authorities work in every state.
-   **Within 1 year:** the Intelligence Act is passed and the parliamentary committee is sitting.
-   **After year 1:** the remaining recruits pass out by month 21, and a quarterly dashboard per state shows vacancies, conviction rates and complaints closed.

## Guardrail ideas

*Ideas to start the conversation, for the builders, experts and the public to shape together.*

-   **Democratic freedoms stay fully protected.** The law defines sabotage narrowly as violence, espionage, terror financing or attack on critical infrastructure, and expressly protects protest, criticism, journalism, strikes and opposition politics.
-   **Every arrest is on camera.** An arrest made with the camera off, without a logged reason, is presumed unlawful unless the police prove otherwise.
-   **Justice arrives on time.** The accused receives bail as of right if the prosecution misses the 90-day trial deadline, with one 60-day extension by a High Court on published reasons.
-   **Every custodial death is examined at once.** A magistrate inquiry follows within 48 hours, and the station head is suspended pending the complaints body's decision.

## FAQs

**Q:** Police is a State subject. Does this take power from the states?

**A:** It is a fair worry, and states come out ahead. They keep full command of their forces and gain better-trained, better-paid police. Standards travel through a model law and grants, willing states can ask Parliament to legislate for them from month 1, and [Chapter 28](/the-third-freedom/locking-in-the-third-freedom) makes police a shared Concurrent subject.

**Q:** Can India hire over 6 lakh people in a year and keep quality high?

**A:** I think we can, if we hold the bar fixed and fill posts in steps. The national cut-off stays high, probation lasts 2 years, extra academy seats come from army and central-force training centres, and the target is 40% on duty by month 12.

**Q:** Will fast special courts convict the innocent?

**A:** It is the right question to ask. Fast courts deliver fair justice sooner for everyone. The 90 days start only after a full charge-sheet, the accused receives a state-paid senior lawyer, and every conviction gets an automatic High Court appeal decided within 90 days.
