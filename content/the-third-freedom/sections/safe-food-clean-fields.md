## The idea

Picture a mother in Lucknow buying milk for her children. She scans the dairy's name on her phone and sees last week's test results, posted within 72 hours by a lab chosen at random. The milk passed. She buys with confidence.

Picture a sweet shop owner in Kolkata who has always sold pure food. The public map shows his clean record week after week, and customers come from across the city.

Picture a farmer in Vidarbha whose village cut its chemical use by a quarter this season. The soil test guided the cut, the Krishi Vigyan Kendra showed him compost and pulse rotation, and his yield held steady. Next season his family takes the next step, and his produce carries the national "Tested Clean" label.

I believe safe food is a basic right, and a country that tests its food openly, every week, earns the trust of its own people and of the world. Europe sets the world's highest bar for what reaches a plate, so I take its bar as ours: a family in Nashik eats food as safe as a family in Munich. Honest traders thrive when testing is visible. Farmers prosper when soil is healthy. And the change to cleaner farming goes as fast as the fields themselves allow, measured block by block.

## Why now

Safe food is a big, reachable prize. Today about **4 in 5** of the food samples we test meet standards. I would like every family to trust the fifth too, and weekly testing in every district can get us there at a modest cost.

Safer chemicals save lives. Pesticide poisoning took **26,921** lives in 2024. Paraquat, a weedkiller with no antidote, is already off the market in many countries, and a national step can protect thousands of families.

Europe's bar is within reach. India registers **318** pesticides, and some, such as metiram and S-metolachlor, are ones the EU banned in 2023. Europe's rapid alert system flagged pesticide residues on Indian products **802** times from 2020 to 2023. Meeting Europe's list at home protects our own families and opens the world's most demanding food market to our farmers.

Organic farming has room to grow. Only about **15 lakh hectares**, around 1% of farmland, is organic today, so every block that moves adds value and premium markets.

And the grain cushion is deep. Our central stocks hold nearly **three times** the buffer norm, so we can move with care and confidence.

## The big moves

Here is the path I suggest, one step at a time.

1.  **First, take Europe's list of approved farm chemicals as India's own.** In the first 30 days the most hazardous pesticides, paraquat among them, give way to safer registered alternatives, named for every crop on day 1. Within 1 year every pesticide and farm chemical not approved in the EU phases out, each with a registered safer alternative named for every crop use, and the companies that sold them buy back every unsold stock. Farmers switch with help already in this book: machines by the hour ([Chapter 11](/the-third-freedom/land-that-works-for-its-owner)), advice from Krishi Vigyan Kendras, and the [Chapter 23](/the-third-freedom/a-hand-up-and-an-equal-chance) transfer for any household under the line. Industrial chemicals in food plants and factories follow Europe's REACH lists of substances that need authorisation or face restriction.
    
2.  **Next, start testing in the open.** We begin with the 100 most populous districts and reach every district within the year, testing milk, oil, spices, sweets and vegetables every week. Any accredited lab can do the work at a published price, and every result appears on a public map within days.
    
3.  **Then, make adulteration the easiest crime to catch and the costliest to commit.** Food fast-track courts decide cases within months, and the heaviest lawful penalties fall on those who knowingly cause harm. A citizen whose tip leads to a conviction shares in the fine.
    
4.  **Within 1 year, hold every plate to Europe's standard.** Imported food meets European residue limits and traceability from month 6, the same rule as every import ([Chapter 7](/the-third-freedom/free-to-build)), and Indian food meets the same EU limits within 1 year. Every residue test appears on the open public dashboard, and any farm that passes a public residue test can use one national "Tested Clean" label.
    
5.  **All along, move to organic at the pace the soil allows.** Volunteer blocks cut chemical use a quarter at a time, starting with the overuse that costs no yield. If yields dip, the block pauses while the soil rebuilds, and the whole transition waits whenever national grain stocks need it (Chapters 11 and 23).

For a modest sum, India gains food it can trust, safer farms and a steady, measured path to organic.

## Proof it works

**Sri Lanka saved lives by replacing toxic pesticides.** Successive bans on the most toxic pesticides from the late 1990s helped bring the suicide rate down from 57 to 17 per 1 lakh people, while farm output held as farmers switched to safer products.

**Europe shows the bar in public.** The EU approves a pesticide only after it is shown safe for people's health, including from residues in food, and free of unacceptable effects on the environment. Every approved and non-approved substance, and every residue limit, sits in one open database anyone can search, with one default limit of 0.01 mg/kg for any pesticide without its own limit. India adopts a proven, published standard on day 1.

**South Korea acted on paraquat.** Suicide deaths by pesticide halved in 2013, the year after paraquat was banned.

**Sikkim went fully organic.** It became India's first fully organic state in 2016, after phasing out chemical inputs over more than a decade.

**Organic yields catch up when soil comes first.** The 40-year Rodale trial in the US found organic yields competitive with conventional after a 5-year transition, and up to 40% higher in drought years. That is why this plan builds soil first and moves step by step.

## What success looks like

-   **In the first 30 days:** the most toxic pesticides and paraquat are replaced, the list of every pesticide not approved in the EU is published with a safer alternative named for each crop use, food courts notified in every state, and weekly testing live in the 100 most populous districts with a public results map.
-   **Within 1 year:** weekly testing runs in every district, food courts are running, and EU-level rules apply to imports from month 6.
-   **Within 1 year:** every pesticide and farm chemical not approved in the EU is phased out, REACH lists apply to industrial chemicals in food plants and factories, and EU residue limits apply to Indian food as well as imports, with every residue test on the open public dashboard.
-   **Within 1 year:** the 25% step runs in volunteer blocks covering at least **1 crore hectares**, with yields published block by block each season.
-   **After year 1:** organic area grows as fast as yields allow, India adopts each new EU decision on a published forward date, and the public gap to Europe's list stays at zero.

## Guardrail ideas

*Ideas to start the conversation, for the builders, experts and the public to shape together.*

-   **Every trader gets a fair re-test.** Every failed test has a sealed counter-sample, re-tested within 15 days at any certified lab. The map shows "provisional" until then and clears the record within 24 hours if the trader is right.
-   **The gravest penalty stays narrow.** It applies only where death and knowledge of lethal harm are proven, at the court's discretion, and only after High Court confirmation.
-   **Small traders get room to fix honest slips.** A first labelling lapse that harms no one gets a warning and 30 days to fix it, with fines scaled to turnover.
-   **Farmers switch with help in hand.** A use leaves the market only once a registered safer alternative is published for it, the registrant buys back every unsold stock at the price paid, and each farm can hire machines by the hour, get Krishi Vigyan Kendra advice and, below the line, the [Chapter 23](/the-third-freedom/a-hand-up-and-an-equal-chance) transfer.
-   **Everyone can plan ahead.** Each new EU decision applies in India from a published future date, every remaining difference from Europe's list sits on a public gap list updated monthly, and approval in the EU opens the door to registration in India through self-registration.
-   **Harvests and households stay secure.** The yield brake and grain lock run automatically, and any household whose income falls below the [Chapter 23](/the-third-freedom/a-hand-up-and-an-equal-chance) line receives that chapter's transfer within 30 days.

## FAQs

**Q:** Will India repeat Sri Lanka's 2021 food crisis?

**A:** India moves at the pace its soil allows. Cuts are 25% a season, in volunteer blocks first, with a yield brake in every block and a national grain lock. Sri Lanka switched overnight; India goes step by step and measures every field.

**Q:** Will farmers have pest control after the bans?

**A:** Farmers gain safer pest control on day 1. A registered alternative is published for every use before any ban applies, and Krishi Vigyan Kendras demonstrate it within 30 days.

**Q:** What does Europe's list cost a farmer?

**A:** Farmers gain healthier families, richer soil and a price premium in the world's most demanding markets. A safer registered alternative is named for every crop use before any change applies, the companies that sold the old chemicals buy back unsold stock, machines are available by the hour ([Chapter 11](/the-third-freedom/land-that-works-for-its-owner)), Krishi Vigyan Kendras show each switch in the field, and any household whose income falls below the line receives the [Chapter 23](/the-third-freedom/a-hand-up-and-an-equal-chance) transfer within 30 days.

**Q:** Why would a block volunteer?

**A:** Volunteering pays. With fertiliser at market price ([Chapter 11](/the-third-freedom/land-that-works-for-its-owner)), cutting overuse saves money, the first cuts cost no yield, and the "Tested Clean" label earns a premium at home and abroad.
