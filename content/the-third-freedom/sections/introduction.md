Let me begin with a girl born today in a village in Bihar, and jump ahead twenty-five years.

She grew up with an AI tutor in her pocket and a science lab in her school. She registered her first company online in an afternoon, raised money from a national fund, and now sells to buyers across India and the world. Her parents own their land on a clean digital record. Her salary arrives in full, with no income tax to file. When she needs a court, it decides in months. When she needs the police, they arrive, well trained and well paid. Her city has clean air, fast trains and homes she can afford.

I believe that India is within reach, in one generation. This book is my attempt to show how we get there.

## Two freedoms won

India has won its freedom twice.

In 1947 it won freedom from foreign rule. In 1991 it won a second freedom, from the licence raj and a closed, state-run economy. The reforms of 1991 opened markets, welcomed investment and let Indians build. Within a generation, hundreds of millions of people rose out of extreme poverty, and India became one of the largest economies in the world.

Each time, energy that had been waiting for a chance was released. Each time, Indians showed that when they are free to build, they build at a scale that surprises the world. I find that the most hopeful fact in our history.

## The third freedom

The third freedom is the freedom of every Indian to reach their full potential, with the state clearing the path and standing behind them.

Think of what that looks like in daily life. Taxes so simple and so low that honesty is the easy choice. A business started with a simple online registration. Courts that work to deadlines, land records everyone trusts, public jobs that reward results, and public money that reaches every person it was meant for.

It means a state that is small where it should be small and strong where it counts. And it means a market where any young Indian with an idea can register a business in a day, raise money, hire, sell anywhere and be judged on results.

## The demographic dividend

India today has the largest and one of the youngest populations on earth. Half of all Indians are under 30. That is the demographic dividend: a once-in-history window in which more people are of working age than at any time before or after.

South Korea, Taiwan and China each turned that window into lasting wealth. India's window is open now, and it is the largest the world has ever seen. The talent is already here, in every town and village. What the third freedom offers is a level, open field for that talent to play on.

Every year, millions of young Indians join the workforce, ready to build. Giving each of them a fair chance at a good job, a business of their own or a world-class skill is, to my mind, the largest opportunity of this century. The reforms in this book are designed to turn that energy into homes, firms, exports and savings within one generation.

## Proven ideas, ready to scale

The ideas in this book are bold, and I have tried to choose only ones already proven in parts, in India or abroad. Two of them may already be on your phone.

-   **Aadhaar** gave more than a billion people a digital identity in under a decade.
-   **UPI** made India the world's largest real-time payments system.
-   **Georgia** rebuilt its police almost overnight and won back its citizens' trust.
-   **Estonia** runs its state online, so citizens deal with government from their phones.
-   **Singapore, South Korea and Shenzhen** went from poor to rich within one working life.

Each chapter tells the stories behind its own ideas in a section called Proof it works.

India has built world-class public systems before, at a scale no other country has matched. So the question is the political will to do all of it together, within one term of government. That is why every action in this book carries one of two clocks: **the first 30 days** or **within 1 year**. Results that naturally take longer, such as a nuclear fleet or a generation of better-educated children, are shown as outcomes after year 1, with the work to start them beginning in year 1.

## Direction to anchor action

No one person can carry all of this out in one lifetime. Not me, not any single leader, not any single government. A change this large needs a group of action-minded builders: people with a bias for action, a strong sense of purpose and the will to deliver results. What I offer is direction: the shape of each reform, why it matters, a clear path to get it done and what success looks like. Think of the recommendations as anchors for the thought, a starting point for that group to debate, sharpen and build on.

That group will include the mission heads who lead each reform, the industry veterans who know each sector from the inside, the entrepreneurs and the private sector that will build much of it, and the citizens who hold it all to account. For them, the detailed blueprints, with the numbers, steps, legal routes and sources, are collected in the **Implementation Annex**. They are a starting draft to refine, one workable way among several.

I am more than happy to play my part in it, in whatever capacity I can be useful. If these ideas move you, I would love to work alongside you.

My hope is simple. I hope you finish each chapter with more ideas than you started with, and a sense that it can be done. The book is written for India, and it offers a path to any country that wants to become developed within this generation.

## How this book is organised

The book opens with the principles that guide every chapter. Part I comes next, because every other reform runs on these money, tax and digital rails. The remaining Parts follow by return on effort: the reforms that cost least and unlock the most growth come first, followed by the heavier, longer-term investments.

| Part | Theme | Why it comes here |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The principles | Ten principles behind every chapter (see [The Principles](/the-third-freedom/the-principles)) | The ideas that every chapter builds on |
| Part I | Money, tax and digital rails | The rails every other reform runs on, built on what India already has (UPI, Aadhaar, GST), plus land records and fast courts |
| Part II | Unleash enterprise | Mostly repeal and deregulation: almost no cost, the fastest growth |
| Part III | Minimum government, maximum governance | Reorganises the state; self-funding through asset sales and savings |
| Part IV | Build the future | Education, capital, defence, sector cities, machines and chips, energy, infrastructure: large investment, long payoff |
| Part V | A liveable India | Clean air and mobility: networks planned for 100 years and paid for on measured results |
| Part VI | People and society | Welfare, food, the internet and social reforms that need careful law |
| Part VII | Safety and the rule of law | Police and the strongest protection for citizens |
| Part VIII | Making it law | The constitutional amendments that lock everything in |

Each chapter follows the same shape:

-   the idea, and the life it makes possible;
-   why now;
-   the big moves;
-   today compared with the new way;
-   proof it works;
-   what success looks like;
-   guardrail ideas;
-   questions and answers.

The Implementation Annex then carries the detailed blueprint for each chapter: the plan, cost and return, timeline, guardrails, legal route, limits and sources.
