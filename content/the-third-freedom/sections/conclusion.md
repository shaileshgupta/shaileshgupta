Go back for a moment to the girl from Bihar we met at the start. Every chapter of this book has been about clearing her path, and it follows one idea: free the people, and make the state excellent at the few things only a state can do.

Every part of it is already proven somewhere. Here is where each one has been shown to work:

| Reform | Proof it works |
| --- | --- |
| Three simple taxes | Countries that run on a single broad GST and light direct taxes |
| A fully digital, cashless economy | UPI, used every day by hundreds of millions of Indians |
| Fast courts | Fast-track courts and the e-courts programme in India; the deadline-driven courts of Singapore |
| A clean, accountable police | Georgia, within a few years |
| A rollout to every citizen | Aadhaar |
| Industry built at speed | Shenzhen, South Korea, and India's own Genome Valley and space cluster |

What is new is doing all of it together, at India's scale, within one term of government. That is the leap this book invites us to imagine.

The reforms reinforce one another, which is why I think they work best as one package:

-   A cashless economy makes a bank transaction tax possible.
-   Digital land records make a fair property tax possible.
-   Retiring income tax brings hidden money into the open, which makes clean public life easier to achieve.
-   One published price and spec, with payment after inspection, makes public works clean and fast.
-   Open markets in land, labour and capital, plus one income-tested cash transfer tied to skills training, give every young Indian both a safety net and a reason to climb.
-   Firm guardrails protect the citizen at every step: published data, deadlines, independent audits and clear consequences for any misuse of power.

The order matters too. Part I lays the money, tax and digital rails that everything else runs on. Part II costs almost nothing and pays back fastest, so it builds the trust and the revenue that the larger reforms need. By the end of year 1 the rules of the game will have changed. The outcomes then build over the years that follow:

-   a generation of better-educated children;
-   a nuclear-powered grid;
-   cities rebuilt at the density they need;
-   an economy in the same league as the United States and China.

Each of those outcomes starts with decisions that can be taken in the first 30 days.

1947 gave India its political freedom. 1991 gave it economic freedom. The third freedom is the freedom of every Indian to reach their full potential, with the state clearing the path and standing behind them.
