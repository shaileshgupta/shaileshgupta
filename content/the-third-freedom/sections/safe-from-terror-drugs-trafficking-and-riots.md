## The idea

Let me start with a mother in Punjab whose son once struggled with drugs. He was found with a small amount, and instead of a prison cell he went to a 90-day treatment programme. He finished it, his record was cleared, and today he works in a local workshop.

Now picture the man who sold him those drugs. His network was traced through the money trail. His house, his cars and the flats in his relatives' names were attached within 30 days of the charge-sheet. His trial ended in 90 days, and he will spend the rest of his life in prison.

And picture a young woman from Jharkhand offered a job in a city. The placement agency is registered, has posted a bond, and pays her against her own PAN, so her wages and her whereabouts stay on the record. She arrives at a real job, safe and paid.

Finally, picture a shopkeeper in a mixed neighbourhood of an old city. One evening a crowd gathers after a rumour spreads online. Within 30 minutes the police are there, cameras rolling, and the crowd melts away. The men who sent the messages and paid the crowd are arrested within days and their assets attached. Had his shop burned, a state fund would have paid him within 30 days and then recovered every rupee from those convicted.

I believe a nation protects its people by being gentle with victims and users, and unbending with organisers, funders and smugglers. Terror, drug trafficking, human trafficking and communal riots are rarely spontaneous. They are organised, funded and led, and they all run on the same fuel: hidden money. One rule covers every religion, caste and language group, so every Indian is protected equally. Follow the money, try the case fast, and the networks come apart.

## Why now

The biggest gain is in turning arrests into convictions. Of more than 6,500 people arrested under the anti-terror law in 2018 to 2022, **252** were convicted. Better investigation and faster trials close that gap, and both can move quickly.

We can also aim higher up the drug chain. Most drug arrests today are for small amounts held for personal use. Sending users to treatment, about **10 lakh** people a year, frees police and prisons to pursue the kingpins and their money.

And the tools are ready. A cashless economy ([Chapter 2](/the-third-freedom/every-rupee-digital)) takes away the main tool of all three crimes, and AI-assisted scanners can check **100%** of containers at every major port within a year.

Peace is worth protecting early too. Communal riots rose by **84%** in 2024, to 59 from 32 the year before (CSSS), while rioting carries up to 2 years today. The Supreme Court ordered rioters to pay for damage in 2009, and Uttar Pradesh and Haryana have built recovery laws, so one national approach can extend that protection to every state.

## The big moves

1.  **First, follow the money.** We start with the fuel. Real-time payment data lets the Financial Intelligence Unit spot suspect patterns, and the assets of the accused, including benami holdings in relatives' names, are attached soon after the charge-sheet, forfeited on conviction and returned with interest on acquittal. Informers share in what is recovered, with their identity protected.
    
2.  **Next, one firm law for terror, for every group and ideology.** Committing, planning, funding or recruiting for terror carries life without remission, and funding counts exactly like the act itself. Trials run in the [Chapter 26](/the-third-freedom/a-police-force-india-can-trust) special courts and finish within 90 days of the charge-sheet.
    
3.  **Then, treatment for users and long sentences for traffickers.** Anyone found with a small amount for personal use goes to treatment and leaves with a clean record. Commercial traffickers face a 20-year minimum, and organisers, smugglers and repeat offenders face life. Scanning every container at our ports closes the front door.
    
4.  **Free and protect every trafficking victim.** Victims receive shelter, the [Chapter 23](/the-third-freedom/a-hand-up-and-an-equal-chance) cash transfer and the skills track. Every placement agency registers on the Business ID, posts a bond and pays workers against their PAN ([Chapter 7](/the-third-freedom/free-to-build)), while organised rings and child traffickers face life.
    
5.  **Bring the same firmness to communal violence, and pay its victims fast.** One national offence covers every religion, caste and language group, with sentences that rise with each person's role. Organising, funding, inciting or leading a riot carries 10 years to life, and life for the rest of one's days where anyone is killed or maimed; taking part carries up to 10 years, and online incitement followed by violence counts as incitement. Every convicted rioter and organiser shares the bill for all the damage, victims are paid from a state fund within 30 days of the damage assessment, and the same special courts try these cases within 90 days, with body-camera, CCTV and phone video and witness protection from the first statement.
    
6.  **Make district leaders own the peace.** Police reach any reported clash within a published time, for example 30 minutes in cities, shown live. Where a riot runs beyond a day, the SP and DM step aside for a quick inquiry, and officers who stood by answer for it in court.
    
7.  **Finally, sentences that mean what they say, after a fair trial.** Life means life, and early release and plea deals are set aside for these crimes. One route to pardon remains through [Chapter 28](/the-third-freedom/locking-in-the-third-freedom): fresh evidence of innocence, examined by a High Court bench. Before conviction, every accused has a lawyer, full disclosure and an automatic appeal.
    

## Today vs the new way

Here is how I see the new approach comparing with today.

| Outcome | Today | The new way |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Terror convictions | 252 from over 6,500 arrests in 2018 to 2022 | Money-trail cases tried within 90 days of the charge-sheet |
| Minimum for commercial drug trafficking | 10 years | 20 years; life for organisers and smugglers |
| Drug users | Arrested and prosecuted | About 10 lakh a year in 90-day treatment (estimate), record cleared |
| Container scanning | Some containers scanned | 100% at every major port and integrated land check post |
| Sentence for organising, funding or leading a riot | Up to 2 years for rioting, 5 if armed; up to 3 years for promoting enmity | 10 years to life; whole life where anyone is killed or maimed |
| Riot victims paid for damage | Recovery laws in a few states, such as UP and Haryana | Every state; paid within 30 days of assessment, with police at any clash within 30 minutes in cities |
| Recruitment agencies | Fake agencies hard to trace | Every agency registered, bonded and paying workers by PAN |
| Criminal wealth | Rarely reached | Attached within 30 days of the charge-sheet, forfeited on conviction |
| Cost | Spread across agencies | About ₹4,500 crore once for scanners and ₹1,000 crore to seed the riot victim fund, and about ₹1,800 crore a year for treatment and courts (estimate), partly repaid by forfeited assets |

The networks and the organisers of riots lose their money and their freedom, and users, victims and every community gain a way back.

## Proof it works

**Whole-life sentences stand in Indian law.** The Supreme Court has upheld life imprisonment without remission imposed by courts, in *Swamy Shraddananda* (2008) and *Union of India v. V. Sriharan* (2015).

**Taking criminal wealth works.** Since 1989, NDPS Chapter VA has forfeited the illegally acquired property of traffickers and their relatives, and the Prevention of Money Laundering Act 2002 allows attachment before trial. Italy's anti-mafia law of 1982 made confiscating mafia assets a core weapon.

**Rioters already pay in some states.** The Supreme Court's 2009 directions, and the recovery tribunals under Uttar Pradesh's 2020 law and Haryana's 2021 law, make rioters pay for the damage they cause. Section 103(2) of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (2023) already punishes murder by a group of five or more on grounds of caste, community, language or belief with death or life imprisonment.

**Treatment for users works.** In 2001 Portugal sent people caught with drugs for personal use to dissuasion commissions and treatment, while trafficking stayed a crime.

## What success looks like

-   **In the first 30 days:** the Zero Tolerance Bill and the Communal Violence (Prevention and Punishment) Bill are tabled, the special courts and 90-day clocks are notified, the scanner specification and price are published, the agency bond registry opens, the riot victim fund is set up, and the police response-time standard is published.
-   **Within 1 year:** every new case, riot cases included, is tried within 90 days of the charge-sheet, and police response times are published live in every district.
-   **Within 1 year:** every container is scanned at every major port and integrated check post, and certified treatment centres operate in every district.
-   **After year 1:** scanning extends to every minor port and land customs station, and quarterly NCRB data by state show convictions rising, communal riots falling and riot victims paid within 30 days.

## Guardrail ideas

*Ideas to start the conversation, for the builders, experts and the public to shape together.*

-   **Every arrest is checked fast.** A charge-sheet is filed within 90 days of arrest, with one 60-day High Court extension at most and bail as of right after that. A magistrate reviews every arrest within 15 days.
-   **The innocent are made whole.** An officer who knowingly files a false case faces up to 7 years in jail, and the wrongly accused receive ₹1 lakh for each month in custody, paid within 30 days.
-   **Surveillance is lawful and logged.** Every warrant comes from a designated High Court judge within 24 hours.
-   **The data is public, and the same rule visibly covers every group.** NCRB publishes arrests, charge-sheets, convictions and acquittals under this law by state every quarter, and riot cases by the group of victims and accused.
-   **Justice is personal and based on evidence.** Liability rests on each person's proven role, after a full trial, and any demolition follows the Supreme Court's November 2024 directions on prior notice and a hearing.
-   **Officers who do their duty are backed.** An SP or DM who met the published response times and deployment rules is reinstated within 30 days.

## FAQs

**Q:** Is life without remission too harsh for every offender?

**A:** I share the instinct for proportion, and the law reflects it. The longest sentences are reserved for those who do the most harm: organisers, funders, cross-border smugglers, repeat offenders and child traffickers. Others face a 20-year minimum, always after a full trial. In a riot, sentences rise with each person's proven role, and those who take part without causing injury face up to 10 years.

**Q:** Could tough laws be turned against innocents and critics?

**A:** That concern deserves a serious answer, and the guardrails give one. They protect every citizen: a 90-day charge-sheet limit, judicial warrants, magistrate review within 15 days, jail for officers who file false cases, compensation for the wrongly accused, and quarterly public data.

**Q:** Could police use the riot law against one community?

**A:** It is a fair concern, given our history. Every community gains the same protection, and the data shows it. Cases are published every quarter by group, body cameras record the evidence, and the [Chapter 26](/the-third-freedom/a-police-force-india-can-trust) complaints authority and faceless audits check every investigation.

**Q:** With early release set aside, what if someone is wrongly convicted?

**A:** This matters to me as much as to any critic. Two routes protect them: an automatic appeal decided within 90 days, and a pardon on fresh evidence of innocence, examined by a High Court bench within 90 days. The death penalty stays a judge's choice in the gravest cases, as the Constitution requires.
