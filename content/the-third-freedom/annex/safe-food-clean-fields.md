## The plan

1.  **Maximum lawful punishment.** Parliament amends the Food Safety and Standards Act and the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita:

| Offence | Punishment |
| --- | --- |
| Adulteration causing grievous harm | Mandatory life imprisonment |
| Adulteration causing death | Life imprisonment, or death in the rarest-of-rare case where the accused knew the adulterant could kill, only after full trial and High Court confirmation |
| On any conviction | Confiscation of the firm's assets and a lifetime ban from the food business for owners and directors |

-   Food fast-track courts finish trials within 180 days; a case where adulteration caused a death is a serious crime and follows the 60-day trial rule in [Chapter 6](/the-third-freedom/justice-on-time).

2.  **Weekly testing in every district.** Each district tests at least 50 samples a week: milk, oil, spices, sweets, street food, fruit and vegetables. Inspectors from another district are assigned by algorithm, wear body cameras and seal a counter-sample. Every result appears on a public map within 72 hours, by shop and brand.
3.  **Open lab network.** Any lab that passes the open spec (NABL accreditation plus blind proficiency samples) is certified, tests anywhere in India at the published price per test and is paid per verified result within 30 days (Aadhaar playbook; How the government buys). Each district gets a rapid-test van.
4.  **Reporter reward.** Anyone whose portal complaint (with a photo or sealed sample) leads to a conviction receives 10% of the fine.
5.  **Europe's list of farm chemicals becomes India's list, in two steps.** The reference is the EU Pesticides Database: every active substance approved under Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009 stays open to registration in India, and every pesticide and farm chemical not approved in the EU phases out.
    -   Within 30 days, ban all pesticides in WHO hazard classes Ia and Ib (extremely and highly hazardous) plus paraquat, under Section 27 of the Insecticides Act. On day 1 the Central Insecticides Board publishes a registered alternative for every crop and pest use, and the full list of pesticides registered in India but not approved in the EU, each with its phase-out date. Registrants buy back stocks within 90 days at their own cost.
    -   Within 1 year, every remaining pesticide and farm chemical not approved in the EU leaves the market, on dates published in the first 30 days, each use only once a registered safer alternative is named for every crop use. Registrants buy back dealer and farm stocks at the price paid, within 90 days of each date, and pay for safe disposal.
    -   Fast entry for safer products: any active substance approved in the EU registers in India by self-registration within 30 days on the EU dossier, with random audits and strict liability, so safer alternatives reach the market quickly.
    -   Farmer support with no subsidy: machine-hire apps ([Chapter 11](/the-third-freedom/land-that-works-for-its-owner)) for mechanical weeding and precision spraying, Krishi Vigyan Kendra field demonstrations of every named alternative within 30 days of each date, and the [Chapter 23](/the-third-freedom/a-hand-up-and-an-equal-chance) transfer for any household under the line.
    -   Industrial chemicals in food plants and factories follow the EU REACH lists: substances on the authorisation list (Annex XIV) are used only under an authorised use, and substances on the restriction list (Annex XVII) follow the same restriction, notified within 1 year.
    -   New EU decisions: each new EU approval, non-approval or restriction is notified in India within 90 days with a published future date, never backdated. A public gap list shows every remaining difference from the EU list, updated monthly.
6.  **EU-level rules for imported food from month 6, and for Indian food within 1 year.** EU pesticide residue limits, with the EU default of 0.01 mg/kg for any pesticide not listed; one-step-back, one-step-forward traceability with a digital certificate naming the farm or plant; document checks on every consignment and lab tests on at least 10%, rising to 50% after two failures and to 100% after three, with the plant barred for 1 year, the same rule as every import ([Chapter 7](/the-third-freedom/free-to-build)); and a public daily rapid-alert list. Importers pay test fees. Within 1 year, Indian food meets the same EU maximum residue limits (set under Regulation (EC) No 396/2005 on EFSA's safety assessment), checked through the weekly district testing above, and every residue result appears on the open public dashboard by farm, brand and shop.
7.  **Organic transition with a yield brake.**
    -   **The step.** Each season, a block's chemical fertiliser and pesticide use falls by 25% of its starting level, beginning with blocks that volunteer. The first cuts remove use above the soil-test dose, which costs no yield. Compost, bio-fertilisers, pulses in rotation and natural-farming methods fill the gap, with advice from Krishi Vigyan Kendras (district farm-science centres).
    -   **The evidence it rests on.** The 40-year Rodale Farming Systems Trial in the US found organic yields competitive with conventional after a 5-year transition, and up to 40% higher in drought years. A global review of 115 studies found the organic yield gap falls from about 19% to 8 to 9% with crop rotation and mixed cropping (Ponisio and others, 2015). Short or poorly managed trials show gaps of about 25% (Seufert and others, 2012), and Sri Lanka's overnight 2021 ban cut rice output by about 20% in six months. So the plan builds soil first, goes step by step, and measures every block.
    -   **The yield brake.** If a block's measured yield falls more than 10% below its 3-year average, cuts stop there for one season. A block that trips the brake twice goes back one step and holds for 2 seasons while soil carbon is rebuilt.
    -   **The grain lock.** The transition pauses nationally if central stocks fall below the buffer norm kept under Chapters 11 and 23 on any quarter date, or if national rice or wheat output falls more than 5% year on year.
    -   **Tested Clean label.** Any farm whose produce passes a public residue test may use one national "Tested Clean" label.

| Rapid-test vans | 500 more rapid-test vans × ₹50 lakh | **250** |  |

| Food courts | about 780 courts × ₹1 crore a year (judge, staff, premises) | ≈ 780 a year | the Centre pays 80% (about ₹620 crore) in year 1 |

-   **Europe's chemical list:** registrants pay for buy-back and disposal of unsold stock; residue tests on Indian food run within the 20 lakh weekly district samples above; farmer support runs on the existing machine-hire, Krishi Vigyan Kendra and [Chapter 23](/the-third-freedom/a-hand-up-and-an-equal-chance) routes. No new public spending (estimate).
-   **Organic transition:** no per-hectare payment, under one country, one law. Farms pay for soil tests like any input (about ₹500 a test, estimate). Yield measurement and advice run through Krishi Vigyan Kendras on the existing extension budget. Nothing is drawn from the [Chapter 23](/the-third-freedom/a-hand-up-and-an-equal-chance) subsidy saving.
-   **Return:** EU residue limits at home open the European market wider to Indian food, which drew 802 EU rapid alerts for pesticide residues in 2020 to 2023; farms that cut overuse save input costs once fertiliser sells at market price ([Chapter 11](/the-third-freedom/land-that-works-for-its-owner)); a trusted label earns a premium at home and abroad; and every pesticide death avoided is a life saved.
-   **Jobs (estimate):** tens of thousands of lab technicians, sample collectors, bio-input makers and certification agents.

## Timeline

**First 30 days:**

-   Ban WHO Class Ia and Ib pesticides and paraquat, with alternatives published on day 1 and a 90-day buy-back paid by registrants.
-   Publish the list of pesticides registered in India but not approved in the EU, with phase-out dates and a named safer alternative for every crop use; open 30-day self-registration for EU-approved substances; launch the public gap list.
-   Notify food fast-track courts in every state.
-   Publish the open lab spec and certification test; certify labs as they pass; launch the public results map.
-   Start weekly testing in the 100 most populous districts.
-   Introduce the food-safety amendments to the FSSA and the BNS.

**Within 1 year:**

-   Weekly testing in all districts.
-   Amendments passed; food courts running.
-   EU REACH authorisation and restriction lists notified for food plants and factories; every pesticide and farm chemical not approved in the EU phased out, with buy-backs complete.
-   EU-level import rules in force from month 6; EU maximum residue limits apply to Indian food, with every residue test on the open public dashboard.
-   The 25% step running in all volunteer blocks, covering at least **1 crore hectares**, with yields published block by block each season.

**Outcomes after year 1:** organic area grows as fast as yields allow, block by block; India adopts each new EU decision on a published forward date, and the gap list stays at zero.

## Guardrails

-   **Labs:** paid within 30 days. More than 2% disputed results in a quarter means de-certification within 7 days.
-   **Re-test right:** every failed sample has a sealed counter-sample the trader can have re-tested within 15 days at any certified lab. The map shows a result as "provisional" until then, and removes it within 24 hours if the counter-sample clears.
-   **Death penalty limits:** never mandatory; only where death and knowledge of lethal harm are proven; only after High Court confirmation.
-   **Small traders:** a first non-harmful labelling lapse gets a warning and 30 days to fix it. Fines scale with turnover.
-   **Reporter abuse:** anyone whose 3 complaints in a year all test clean loses reward eligibility for 2 years; a knowingly false complaint is fined the amount of the reward claimed.
-   **Yield brake:** yield is measured by crop-cutting experiments and satellite data; a fall of more than 10% stops cuts in that block automatically.
-   **Grain lock:** no expansion while stocks are below the buffer norm or national rice or wheat output is down more than 5%.
-   **Courts:** a state that misses the 180-day trial target for 2 quarters loses the central share of court funding.
-   **Uptake:** if volunteer blocks cover less than 50 lakh hectares at month 6, Krishi Vigyan Kendra field demonstrations double in the lagging districts.
-   **Alternative first:** no use of a pesticide ends until a registered safer alternative for that crop use is published; a registrant that misses a buy-back date pays 2 times the stock value to the affected dealers and farmers.
-   **No surprises:** every phase-out date is published at least 90 days ahead and never applies backwards; the gap list with Europe is published monthly.
-   **Household safety net:** any household whose income falls below the [Chapter 23](/the-third-freedom/a-hand-up-and-an-equal-chance) line gets the [Chapter 23](/the-third-freedom/a-hand-up-and-an-equal-chance) transfer within 30 days, on the same rule as everyone.

## Limits to know

A ban on all chemical fertiliser within 1 year cannot be done safely; yields need time as soil is rebuilt. The path through is the 25% step with the yield brake and grain lock, so organic becomes the norm wherever the measured data supports it.

The EU list is written for Europe's crops and pests, so a few Indian crop uses (tropical pests, for example) may have no EU-approved product within 1 year. The path through follows the EU's own emergency authorisations under Article 53 of Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009: a time-limited use, named crop by crop, shown on the gap list with a fixed end date. EU approval takes about 4 years on average, so India relies on the EU's published decisions instead of repeating them. The Insecticides Act is a central law, so the phase-out runs nationally under one law.
