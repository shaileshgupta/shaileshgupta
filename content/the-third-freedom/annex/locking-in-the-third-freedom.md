## The rules of amendment

-   **Article 368 sets three levels.** (a) A simple-majority law, where the Constitution itself lets Parliament act by ordinary law. (b) A special majority: two-thirds of members present and voting and a majority of the total membership, in each House. (c) A special majority plus ratification by at least half the state legislatures, for federal subjects: the Seventh Schedule, Articles 245 to 255, the GST Council (Article 279A), the higher courts, the President's election and representation of states in Parliament.
-   **The numbers.** Lok Sabha 543: two-thirds is 362 if every member votes. Rajya Sabha 245: two-thirds is 164 if every member votes, and a majority of total membership is 123. With 28 states, "not less than one-half" means **14 ratifications**; union territories do not count.
-   **Today's strength.** The NDA won 293 Lok Sabha seats in 2024 and had about 152 Rajya Sabha members in June 2026. It governs 21 of the 28 states (May 2026). Ratification is within reach; two-thirds in both Houses needs allies.
-   **Procedure matters.** In 2021 the Supreme Court struck down most of Part IXB (the 97th Amendment on cooperatives) because it changed state subjects without state ratification.
-   **Speed is possible.** The 103rd Amendment (EWS quota) passed both Houses in 2 days, 323 to 3 in the Lok Sabha. The 101st Amendment (GST) cleared Parliament in August 2016, Assam ratified first on 12 August, and the President signed on 8 September 2016.

2.  **Three bills on day 1 of a special session** called under Article 85:

| Bill | Procedure | What it carries |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Bill A** | special majority only | Article 20(4), Articles 72/161, 310/311, 312, 15/16, the 371 series |
| **Bill B** | special majority plus ratification | all Seventh Schedule moves, Articles 246A/279A, Parts IX/IXA, Articles 330/332/334 |
| **Act C** | ordinary law | income-tax repeal, the BTT, the civil code, the Fifth and Sixth Schedule changes, the political-funding law ([Chapter 13](/the-third-freedom/clean-money-trusted-public-life)) |

-   Every bill has a severability clause and is voted schedule by schedule, so one failed item does not sink the rest, and a slow state never holds up Bill A.

3.  **Bridges while ratification runs.** Article 249 lets Parliament legislate on any State List subject for 1 year at a time if the Rajya Sabha resolves so by two-thirds of members present and voting. Article 252 lets two or more willing states ask Parliament to legislate for them. These carry land, police standards and stamp-duty reform in willing states from month 1.
4.  **Ratification in 60 days.** Bill B goes to all 28 legislatures the day Parliament passes it. Each state cabinet is asked to advise its Governor to summon a 1-day sitting (Article 174) within 30 days. The target is 15 ratifications by day 60, one more than needed, with NDA states first, as Assam did for GST. Every legislature votes within 1 year.
5.  **The coalition, item by item.** In the Lok Sabha, 362 needs about 70 votes beyond the NDA's 293. In the Rajya Sabha, 164 needs about 12 beyond about 152. The legal floor is lower when members abstain (two-thirds of those voting, and at least 272 and 123), but the plan aims for full Houses. GST and the EWS amendment passed with cross-party votes because states and voters gained; each schedule here is built the same way.

## Cost and return

-   **Special session (estimate):** Parliament costs about ₹2.5 lakh a minute to run (2012 figure). 15 sitting days × 6 hours × 60 minutes × ₹2.5 lakh ≈ **₹135 crore**, negligible next to the reforms it enables.
-   **States are held whole.** States lose about ₹1.7 lakh crore of stamp duty and about ₹1.5 lakh crore of vehicle tax and electricity duty ([Chapter 1](/the-third-freedom/tax-keep-what-you-earn) estimates). They gain 100% of the 0.4% property tax (about ₹3.8 lakh crore, [Chapter 1](/the-third-freedom/tax-keep-what-you-earn)), the state per-unit fuel, alcohol and tobacco levies and 50% of GST. Any remaining gap is paid by the Centre state by state, as in [Chapter 1](/the-third-freedom/tax-keep-what-you-earn).
-   **Compensation backstop**, modelled on Section 18 of the 101st Amendment: any state whose year-1 revenue falls below its FY26 base is paid the gap monthly, counted after property tax and its GST share. The backstop runs for the first 12 months after switch day ([Chapter 1](/the-third-freedom/tax-keep-what-you-earn)), the year in which stamp duty and the other state taxes end, and has no extension clause. Any residual comes from the BTT pool and is published monthly.
-   **Tracker:** a public site, built by NIC in 30 days, shows every clause, every named vote and every state's ratification status.

## Timeline

**First 30 days:**

-   Days 1 to 5: publish all three bills with a one-page plain-English note per clause; convene the special session.
-   Day 1: a Presidential reference to the Supreme Court under Article 143 on the four Medium-risk items (three taxes only, pardon, quotas, police). Parliament does not wait for the opinion.
-   Days 6 to 15: a Joint Parliamentary Committee reports clause by clause.
-   Days 16 to 25: votes on Bills A and B and Act C; the Article 249 resolution.
-   Days 26 to 30: Bill B sent to the states; Act C notified.

**Within 1 year:**

-   15 or more ratifications by day 60; assent and notification after the 14th.
-   Every remaining legislature votes by month 12.
-   Article 249 laws replaced by Concurrent-List laws once Bill B is in force.
-   The Union asks for any challenge to be heard by a Constitution Bench within 90 days.

## Guardrails

-   **Bright line:** no clause may touch judicial review (Articles 32, 136, 226), the core of Articles 14, 19 and 21, or free and fair elections. The Law Ministry certifies this in writing for every clause before tabling.
-   Every clause is published **14 days** before its vote, with a named minister as owner.
-   Recorded named votes on every schedule, published within 24 hours.
-   Nothing is placed in the Ninth Schedule as a shield; after *I.R. Coelho* (2007) it gives no protection.
-   State revenue guarantee paid by the **10th of each month**; a late payment adds 1% a month interest owed to the state.
-   Any state that has not voted by month 6 is named on the tracker with its scheduled date.
-   An independent review by 3 retired Supreme Court judges, published within 30 days of the first anniversary.

## Limits to know

The basic structure doctrine (*Kesavananda Bharati*, 1973; *Minerva Mills*, 1980) limits even a unanimous Parliament. Removing judicial review, removing fundamental rights wholesale and executive control over judges cannot be done by amendment; the plan uses the strongest versions that survive (Chapters 6, 26 and 27). Two-thirds of both Houses is political arithmetic, not law: about 70 more Lok Sabha votes and 12 more Rajya Sabha votes are needed today.
