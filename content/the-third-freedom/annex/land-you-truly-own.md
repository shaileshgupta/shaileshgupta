## The plan

1.  **One mission, one leader.** A National Land and Law Records Mission is led by a tech czar with cabinet-minister rank, on a fixed 1-year term with published targets, reporting directly to the Prime Minister (the [Chapter 12](/the-third-freedom/a-lean-state-that-delivers) mission rule). A council of Chief Ministers advises but does not direct. Land and revenue records are state subjects, so the mission acts in each state through an Article 252 law (states that opt in), the Article 249 bridge, and then [Chapter 28](/the-third-freedom/locking-in-the-third-freedom)'s Bill B, which moves land titles to the Concurrent List.
2.  **The 30-day "finish and join" sprint, for land and law:**
    -   scan every remaining paper land, registration and revenue-court file;
    -   turn every record into structured data: owner, ULPIN, area, encumbrances (any loan, lien or claim on the land) and the linked court case number;
    -   publish every central and state law, rule and notification as searchable text.
3.  **Court records within 1 year.** Files of all pending cases are scanned and linked by month 6, so the AI courts ([Chapter 6](/the-third-freedom/justice-on-time)) can work from them; all legacy records are done by month 12. The Supreme Court's e-Committee issues the directive, and each High Court sets the deadlines for its courts under its own administrative power (Articles 227 and 235). The work is paid from the existing e-Courts Phase III budget, brought forward from 2027.
4.  **Open specs, not a tender.** The mission publishes the record specification, a certification test and one fixed price per page and per structured record. The price is the median of three independent cost consultancies' estimates (How the government buys). Any firm, large or small, that passes the test and posts the 10% performance bond can take work packages in any district, first come first served within district quotas that shift each quarter toward the best audited quality. This follows the Aadhaar playbook: certified operators whose ID is stamped on every record, payment per verified record, and de-certification within 7 days for a failed audit.
5.  **Pay on verification.** A record is paid only after duplicates are removed and personal data is masked for public view. 70% is paid within 30 days of passing automated checks, and the remaining 30% within 90 days of a 5% random human audit by a different certified firm (stricter than the playbook's 2%, because these are legal records).
6.  **Guaranteed titles within 1 year.** A Land Titling Act, based on the 2020 draft model law released by NITI Aayog, converts each joined-up record into a **guaranteed title**:
    -   a 6-month public objection window opens, with an SMS to every name on the record (an owner who misses it is still protected by the fund below);
    -   an uncontested record that matches the drone map becomes conclusive;
    -   anyone later proven to be the true owner is paid market value from a **Title Guarantee Fund**, and the titleholder keeps the land.

States adopt the model Act until Bill B is in force. From then, Parliament passes one national Land Titling Act for every state (one country, one law), and the state Acts fold into it.

7.  **Every claim counts, one rule for all.** Anyone with an unrecorded or customary claim, including claims under the Forest Rights Act, can file it in the same objection window at any tehsil or on the portal. Every forest village holds a gram sabha filing camp inside the window. A title with no claim becomes conclusive the day the window closes; a title with a claim becomes conclusive once that claim is decided, within 90 days plus one appeal in 30. From then on the title is never reopened: any later claim, including a Forest Rights claim, is settled only by payment at market value from the Title Guarantee Fund. Sixth Schedule areas follow their district councils' land laws until the Constitution is amended; the same titling law is offered to those councils to adopt.

## Cost and return

**30-day land sprint (estimate):**

| Item | Working | Cost (₹ crore) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Scanning | about 150 crore remaining land, registration and revenue pages × ₹1.5 a page (a typical state scanning rate) | ≈ 225 |
| Structured records | about 60 crore structured records (37 crore parcels plus about 23 crore deeds and case links) × ₹10 | ≈ 600 |
| Audit and government cloud |  | about 175 |
| **Total** | 225 + 600 + 175 | **≈ 1,000** |

-   **Court records within 1 year (estimate):** about 2,450 crore pages (3,100 crore less the 660 crore done) × ₹1.5 ≈ **₹3,700 crore**, met first from the e-Courts Phase III budget, which already covers record digitisation.
-   **Capacity check (estimate):** the land sprint needs about 5 crore pages a day (150 crore ÷ 30), or about 1.7 lakh operators at 300 pages a day each, given fragile old registers. Court files need about 7.4 crore pages a day over about 330 working days, or about 74,000 operators on production scanners at 1,000 pages a day. India's IT and business-process industry can field both. Firms bring their own devices, and the cost sits inside the per-page price.
-   **Title Guarantee Fund:** stamp duty and registration fees are abolished ([Chapter 1](/the-third-freedom/tax-keep-what-you-earn)), so no new levy is added. Each adopting state's fund takes 0.5% of the property tax collected in that state: across all states, 0.5% × about ₹3.8 lakh crore ([Chapter 1](/the-third-freedom/tax-keep-what-you-earn)) ≈ **₹1,900 crore a year** (estimate).
-   **Benefit (estimate):** guaranteed titles let banks lend against land with confidence. If 5% of the 37 crore parcels raise an average loan of ₹2 lakh, that is 1.85 crore × ₹2 lakh = **₹3.7 lakh crore** of new secured credit. Fewer land suits also free court time.
-   **Technology:** ULPIN as the key, the national land data APIs, OCR and AI extraction followed by a human check, the government cloud (MeghRaj), links to e-Courts and the national judicial data grid, and digital signatures.
-   **Jobs (estimate):** about 1.7 lakh short-term jobs for the 30-day sprint and about 74,000 for the year-long court work.

## Timeline

**First 30 days:**

-   Day 1: the czar is appointed. Day 3: the specification, certification test and rate card are published.
-   Day 5: certified firms start in every district, with their sealed devices registered with the mission. A live dashboard shows pages done in every office.
-   Day 30: every land and revenue office below 100% gets a 7-day notice. Every law is online as searchable text.
-   The Supreme Court e-Committee directive on court records is issued, and High Courts publish their court-by-court deadlines.

**Within 1 year:**

-   Month 6: all pending-case court files are scanned and linked.
-   Month 12: all legacy court records are done and linked to land records; every record is joined to its ULPIN and map in every state; registration updates ownership automatically everywhere.
-   Month 12: at least 20 states have adopted the Land Titling Act, the first objection windows close, and the first guaranteed titles are issued.

**Outcomes after year 1:**

-   Objection windows close in the remaining states as each adopts the Act or the national Act takes effect under Bill B. Every claim filed in a window is decided within 90 days, with one appeal in 30.

## Guardrails

-   **Accuracy:** field accuracy of 99.5% or more on the audit sample is paid in full. Between 98% and 99.5%, the firm fixes every error at its own cost within 15 days before it is paid. Below 98%, the firm repays and is suspended for 30 days; a second time below 98% means de-certification for 1 year.
-   **Office heads:** after the 7-day notice, a tehsildar or registrar who misses the deadline is transferred out within 24 hours and suspended pending inquiry; dismissal follows the Article 311 process within 90 days. For court record rooms, the High Court applies the same deadline to its own staff under Article 235.
-   **Privacy:** public view shows only ULPIN, area and owner initials. Full data is shown only to the owner, to banks with the owner's consent, and to courts.
-   **No silent edits:** every change is logged with the officer's ID, and the owner gets an SMS within 1 hour of any change to the record.
-   **Device security:** devices are firm-owned, registered by day 5, sealed (no network, encrypted storage, remote wipe), kept in government custody, and wiped under a CERT-In-empanelled auditor's check before release. A broken seal forfeits the firm's bond; each data leak costs ₹1 crore.
-   **Objections:** an objection needs an identity check and documents. The first objection per plot is free; later ones cost ₹1,000, refunded if upheld. The tehsil decides in 90 days, with one appeal decided in 30. A proven false objection costs ₹50,000 and is a criminal case.
-   **People who could not object:** minors and people lacking mental capacity can claim compensation until 3 years after the incapacity ends. Every inheritance change needs the full list of legal heirs from the succession certificate or family register, and each heir gets an SMS.
-   **Title Guarantee Fund:** a valid claim needs a court decree and is paid at market value within 180 days. The fund then recovers from the fraudster and any official who altered the record. Payouts above 3 times the fund's yearly income are paid in up to 3 yearly instalments with interest at the repo rate, so the liability is known each year.
-   **Czar accountability:** removable only by the Prime Minister, with written reasons published within 7 days; a quarterly report to Parliament; an independent CAG audit published within 90 days of the term's end. The term is renewed each year only if the czar met at least 4 of 5 published targets, with fresh 1-year targets each time.

## Limits to know

Court records are physically too large for 30 days: about 2,450 crore pages remain, so they get 12 months, with pending cases first. The executive cannot direct judges or court staff, so the court-records half depends on the Supreme Court e-Committee directive and each High Court's own orders, which the courts can issue administratively.

Final titles trade one thing for certainty: a person who misses the window gets money, not the land itself. For forest communities whose life is tied to their land, money is a weaker remedy, which is why the gram sabha filing camps come to every forest village inside the window.
