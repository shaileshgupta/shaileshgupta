## The plan

1.  **A 1-year last-mile target.**
    -   100% of broad-gauge rail electrified.
    -   Every household connected and metered with a meter certified to the open spec, with 24-hour supply on urban feeders and at least 22 hours on rural feeders by month 12, published live per feeder.
    -   Every unconnected habitation of 250 people or more, under one rule for the whole country, gets its all-weather road published as a work package in 30 days, and every package is claimed and under way within 1 year.
    -   Every national highway bridge older than 25 years is inspected, and every bridge rated "poor" gets strain sensors.
2.  **All works use the national rate card (How the government buys).** The government publishes every road, line, connection and bridge still needed, by district, as work packages at one national price per unit of finished work. The price is the median of three independent cost consultancies, with only three formula adjustments: terrain (up to +10%), a state labour and material index (±5%) and monthly indexation for steel, cement, bitumen and diesel. There is no bid and no negotiation. A package unclaimed for 30 days gets +5%, up to +10%.
3.  **Any certified contractor claims a package by posting the bond upfront.**
    -   The bond is **10%** of package value, or **20%** for packages of ₹100 crore or more.
    -   It can be any mix of bank guarantee, insurance surety bond and fixed deposit.
    -   Certification has no turnover or years-in-business threshold, only the construction spec test and the bond, so startups and small firms can enter every field. A contractor holds only as many packages as its bonds and audited record cover.
4.  **An independent test before every payment.** Software picks a NABL-accredited lab at random to test materials and finished work at each milestone. Results go on a public geotagged portal within 72 hours, with drone survey images. No pass, no payment. Large packages are paid per inspected milestone; small ones on completion. Payment is made within 30 days of a pass.
5.  **A 10-year defect warranty.** The contractor fixes any structural defect for 10 years, backed by compulsory latent-defect insurance, as with France's 10-year "décennale" cover. GIC Re reinsures all such cover written in year 1. Where cover is not yet available, half of the bond stays in place for 10 years instead.
6.  **Bans that stick (Public Works Accountability Act).**
    -   Trigger: a structural failure caused by proven substandard work, found by a lab or forensic report, after a hearing.
    -   The firm is barred from public work for 10 years, reviewable after 5 only if every repair cost and damage award is paid.
    -   Promoters and directors convicted after trial are barred for life, in any firm where they hold 10% or more or a board seat, traced by PAN and DIN.
    -   Assets are seized by court order, as civil recovery, up to the cost of repair plus compensation plus a 2× penalty.
7.  **Jail for the engineer who signs off unsafe work.** A Professional Engineers Act certifies every engineer who certifies public works and makes professional liability insurance compulsory. A new offence of certifying unsafe public works, knowing or having reason to know it was unsafe, carries up to 10 years in jail if lives are lost and up to 3 years otherwise. An engineer who flagged the defect in writing on the portal before signing is not liable for it.
8.  **City redevelopment by resident choice.**
    -   **Private land:** a ward or zone is offered for redevelopment only when owners of at least 70% of the floor area, and at least 70% of affected families, consent on the land registry ([Chapter 5](/the-third-freedom/land-you-truly-own)). The deal is published first: the builder rehouses every resident and tenant to spec and hands the city a fixed share of the new floor area, set by the three cost consultancies and never negotiated. Residents then vote between certified builders who accept that deal. Holdouts are acquired under the 2013 land acquisition law at market value from the [Chapter 1](/the-third-freedom/tax-keep-what-you-earn) area index plus the Act's 100% solatium, decided within 90 days.
    -   **Government land:** here the state is selling a scarce asset, so the site is sold by open e-auction to the certified builder offering the city the largest share of new floor area, above a reserve set by the consultancies. The rehousing spec is fixed and not part of the bid.
    -   Every resident and tenant is rehoused within 1 km with at least equal floor area, written into the contract.

### Plan once, dig once: one map, one 100-year plan, shared ducts

9.  **One underground map.** Every utility (water, sewer, drains, power, gas, fibre, street lighting) uploads its network to one GIS layer on PM Gati Shakti, linked to Call Before u Dig, within 30 days. A ground-penetrating radar survey of every urban arterial road completes the map within 1 year. A line not on the map after day 30 gets no compensation if it is damaged.
10.  **Dig-once rule.** A road may not be cut for 5 years after it is built or resurfaced. Every planned dig is booked on one city portal at least 30 days ahead. The booking alerts every other utility, which lays its pipes or ducts in the same open trench in the same window, or uses the ducts later. Emergency cuts are allowed with a geotagged photo, are closed within 72 hours, and are restored to specification at the cutter's cost.
11.  **Common ducts in every new or rebuilt road.**

| Road width (m) | What goes under it |
| --- | --- |
| Wider than 18 | a walkable utility tunnel where 4 or more utilities share the corridor, otherwise a common duct bank |
| 9 to 18 | a common duct bank with at least 4 spare conduits |
| Narrower | one shared trench with spare conduits |

-   Crossing sleeves every 50 m and side ducts to each plot, so a new house connection needs no road cut.
-   The city owns the ducts. Any certified operator rents spare space at a published rent per duct-km, first come first served, and pulls cable through without digging.

12.  **One master plan per city in 1 year.** Every city of 1 lakh people or more (about 600 cities, estimate) adopts one plan covering land use, roads and every utility, with a 5-year works calendar for each street. Plans follow one national specification and are drawn by certified planning firms at a published price per city (How the government buys). Each plan gets 30 days of public comment and is approved by the city CEO ([Chapter 14](/the-third-freedom/leaders-who-run-their-areas-like-ceos)) within the next 30 days. It is revised every year.
     -   **100-year growth frame:** each plan has three layers: a 100-year structure plan (projected population and jobs, the full road, transit, utility and green network the city will need), a 20-year development plan, and the 5-year works calendar. The structure plan is reviewed every 10 years; its corridors change only by a two-thirds vote of the city council with published reasons.
     -   **Reserved corridors:** rights of way for future arterial roads, metro and rail, utility trunk lines, drainage and parks are mapped, published and recorded on every affected title in the land registry ([Chapter 5](/the-third-freedom/land-you-truly-own)). No permanent structure is permitted on them. Owners keep using the land for temporary uses and receive market value or transfer of development rights when the corridor is built.
     -   **Sized for the future:** trunk water mains, trunk sewers, storm drains and duct banks are designed for the 100-year peak flow; distribution lines, treatment plants and pumps are added in modules as demand grows. The national specification publishes standard design factors so every city sizes the same way.
13.  **Below ground first.** A stretch is resurfaced only after every utility work in its calendar is finished. The road contractor's 10-year defect warranty (item 5) covers the trench too.
14.  **One Utility Coordination Office per city**, under the city CEO, issues every dig permit and refuses any dig that breaks the calendar.

## Cost and return

-   **Market:** at least ₹17.1 lakh crore a year of public capital spending, central and state-supported.

| Line (estimate) | Arithmetic | Amount | Who pays |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Savings: halving the 20.7% overrun on the current pipeline | ₹5.71 lakh crore × 50% | ≈ **₹2.9 lakh crore**, about ₹57,000 crore a year over a 5-year build cycle |  |
| Testing: 0.3% of project value | ₹17.1 lakh crore × 0.3% | ≈ **₹5,100 crore a year**, under a tenth of the savings | the project owner, from project budgets |
| Bonds and insurance, built into the rate card | surety premium about 1% a year on the bonded amount + latent-defect insurance of about 0.5% of value | roughly 1.5% of value, far below the 20.7% average overrun | contractors |
| Underground map: radar survey plus GIS layer and booking portal, one-time | about 1 lakh km of urban arterials × ₹20,000 per km = ₹200 crore, plus about ₹300 crore | ≈ **₹500 crore** |  |
| Common ducts, paid at the national rate card | about 10,000 km of new and rebuilt urban roads in year 1 × ₹50 lakh per km | ≈ **₹5,000 crore** |  |
| Utility tunnels, started in year 1 | the 500 busiest km × ₹20 crore per km | ≈ ₹10,000 crore |  |
| City master plans | 600 cities × ₹2 crore | ≈ **₹1,200 crore** |  |
| Revenue: duct rent to cities, once built out | 600 cities × 100 km of ducts × 4 spare conduits × ₹1 lakh per duct-km a year | ≈ **₹2,400 crore a year** | certified operators renting space |

-   **Bridge sensors (estimate):** about ₹10 lakh per major bridge.
-   **Dig-once savings (estimate):**
    -   Halving fibre cuts (about 10 lakh a year, about ₹3,000 crore lost) saves about ₹1,500 crore a year.
    -   If a city road is cut about 3 times between resurfacings, and each cut-and-patch costs about 15% of a resurfacing, repeat cuts add about 45% to each resurfacing cycle. Dig-once avoids most of that.
    -   Telecom and power rollouts move faster: cable is pulled through ducts in days instead of waiting months for permits.
-   **Building for 100 years (estimate):** laying trunk pipes and ducts for the 100-year peak adds about 10% to 15% to their cost, because excavation, not pipe, is most of it; replacing an undersized trunk line later costs the full project again plus the disruption. Every corridor kept free today avoids acquiring built-up land later at many times the price.
-   **Who builds the market:** IRDAI lets insurers use a larger share of their solvency margin for surety and GIC Re reinsures year-1 cover. About 1,500 NABL labs plus new private labs do the testing. Certified survey firms, planning firms and duct contractors build the city works under the Aadhaar playbook and the national rate card: published work packages, bond posted upfront, payment only after independent inspection (How the government buys).
-   **Jobs (estimate):** 10,000 or more testing and inspection jobs, and steadier work for India's construction workforce.

## Timeline

**First 30 days:**

-   A Finance Ministry order sets the 10% and 20% upfront bond for all new work, accepts surety bonds and fixed deposits everywhere, and ends lowest-bid tenders for works.
-   The draft national rate card is published from today's CPWD, MoRTH and Jal Jeevan Mission schedules of rates.
-   The last-mile gap list is published by district (unelectrified rail km, unconnected habitations, unmetered households), cut into work packages.
-   The random lab-allocation portal goes live; every new milestone payment needs a lab pass.
-   Every bridge flagged "poor" in the Indian Bridge Management System is inspected, with public results within 30 days.
-   Publish the Model Dig-Once Act for states, with 20% of central urban grants tied to adoption. Use central powers at once for telecom (Telecommunications Act 2023), power (Electricity Act 2003) and gas (PNGRB Act 2006).
-   Every utility uploads its existing maps; the dig booking portal goes live on Call Before u Dig.
-   Add duct bank, utility tunnel and crossing sleeve items to the national rate card.
-   Publish the master-plan specification, certification test and price per city.

**Within 1 year:**

-   100% broad-gauge rail electrification, every household metered, and the feeder-hour targets met, tracked monthly on the scorecard ([Chapter 14](/the-third-freedom/leaders-who-run-their-areas-like-ceos)).
-   Every habitation road package claimed and under way.
-   Every national highway bridge older than 25 years inspected.
-   The Public Works Accountability Act and the Professional Engineers Act passed, including the new offence.
-   Latent-defect insurance compulsory for all public structures.
-   The first 10 city redevelopment zones assigned.
-   Radar survey of every urban arterial complete; the underground map becomes the single record.
-   Master plans, each with its 100-year growth frame, adopted in every city of 1 lakh or more.
-   Every new or rebuilt urban road built with ducts; the first 50 utility tunnels under construction.
-   The 5-year no-cut rule in force in every adopting state.

**Outcomes after year 1:**

-   Remaining habitation roads completed under the same rules.
-   New national corridors built at the rate card, with every package bonded, tested and warrantied.
-   Full 24-hour power on every feeder, tracked monthly.
-   Utility tunnels on the 500 busiest km completed; duct rent reaching about ₹2,400 crore a year (estimate).

## Guardrails

-   Payment within 30 days of a test pass. If the government pays late, it owes 1.5% interest a month (Aadhaar playbook).
-   5% of tests are re-run by a second random lab; a mismatch of more than 10% triggers an audit of both labs. A lab that falsifies a result loses accreditation for life, and its signatory faces the same offence as the engineer.
-   Ban process: show-cause notice, hearing within 30 days, decision within 60 days, appeal to a tribunal decided within 90 days.
-   Asset seizure only by court order after the finding, capped at repair cost plus compensation plus a 2× penalty.
-   Redevelopment: no eviction until the new home has an occupancy certificate. If handover is late, the builder pays rent at market rate plus 20%.
-   Cost per km and per unit of every package is published on the scorecard, so any price rise shows at once.
-   **European bar for design:** the Eurocodes (EN 1990 to EN 1999) are the reference level for structural design. Within 1 year, every Indian code is compared with them, the gaps are published, and each gap carries a date to close; the comparison is republished every year.
-   **Unbooked cut:** a fine of 10 times the restoration cost, paid by the utility within 30 days; the engineer who approved it loses dig-permit rights for 1 year.
-   **Emergency cuts:** more than 2 per km per year by one utility triggers a faceless audit ([Chapter 13](/the-third-freedom/clean-money-trusted-public-life)) within 30 days.
-   **Map accuracy:** each strike on a wrongly mapped line costs that line's owner ₹1 lakh; each utility's error rate is published monthly.
-   **Duct rent:** set by three independent cost consultancies, reviewed every 6 months and published per city. An operator refused space for more than 7 days gets it by order of the city CEO.
-   **Corridors:** any permanent structure on a reserved corridor is removed at the builder's cost, and the official who approved it loses office. Every corridor change is published with reasons 30 days before the vote.
-   **Master plan:** a city that misses the 1-year deadline loses its 20% urban grant until the plan is adopted.

## Limits to know

Building complete new national networks everywhere in one year is not physically possible: highway capacity is about 10,000 to 13,000 km a year. The path is to finish the last mile in year 1 and put every new project under these accountability rules from day 30.
