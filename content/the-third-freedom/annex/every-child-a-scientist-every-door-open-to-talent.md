## The plan

**Schools**

1.  **A daily STEM block.**

| Classes | Maths (minutes a day) | Reading in the mother tongue (minutes a day) | Science (minutes a day) | Coding and computational thinking (minutes a day) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 to 2 | 60 | 60 | taught through stories and play | taught "unplugged", without screens |
| From 3 | 60 |  | 30 | 30 |

2.  **The legal route.** Education is on the Concurrent List (List III, Entry 25). Parliament amends the Right to Education Act, 2009 to set the daily STEM block and national tests for every school. Samagra Shiksha funds (the central school scheme) flow to states on adoption, and states that adopt in the first 6 months get a 20% top-up.
3.  **An AI tutor in all 22 scheduled languages.**
    -   It is part of schooling, on school tablets and DIKSHA, the national digital learning platform, so it is free under the Article 21A right to free schooling (ages 6 to 14).
    -   It works offline on a small on-device model, syncs weekly, and adapts to each child's level.
    -   It is built on an open standard, like UPI. Any firm whose tutor passes the certification test can serve any school in India (Aadhaar playbook).
4.  **National census tests in classes 3, 5 and 8,** taken by every child, not a sample. No child passes or fails. Each school's result is published as learning gain, adjusted for its children's starting level.
5.  **New management for failing schools.**
    -   The bottom 10% of schools by learning gain get a 90-day improvement plan.
    -   If they do not improve on a re-test, a top-performing school, non-profit or operator certified to the open school-management spec runs them on a 3-year contract paid per unit of learning gain.
    -   Fees stay zero (Article 21A).
    -   Teachers keep their jobs under the operator's day-to-day management and the same [Chapter 12](/the-third-freedom/a-lean-state-that-delivers) rule as every public employee: a 90-day improvement window and an appeal.
6.  **Teacher support.** Scripted daily lessons from NCERT, 50 hours of online training for every teacher, and one maths specialist for each cluster of schools.

**Universities**

7.  **Open without approval.** Any institution registers online within 30 days by filing a disclosure. Parliament amends Section 22 of the UGC Act, 1956 in its first session, so a registered disclosure replaces approval for awarding degrees. Until then, new entrants use the deemed-university and graded-autonomy routes.
8.  **Audited outcomes every year.** Each institution publishes its placement rate and median salary at 6 and 12 months, checked against EPFO and bank-inflow data ([Chapter 1](/the-third-freedom/tax-keep-what-you-earn)), plus fees, faculty and dropout rates.
9.  **Student loans follow outcomes.** Government-backed loans go only to institutions whose outcomes are verified. A new institution qualifies by holding 20% of every fee in escrow until its first audited outcome table. The escrow is released to it with interest once the table is verified, or refunded to students if the table is false or the institution closes. Need is covered by the [Chapter 23](/the-third-freedom/a-hand-up-and-an-equal-chance) transfer, not by category scholarships.
10.  **Foreign campuses** enter automatically under the same disclosure rule.

**Talent and tourism**

11.  **A 10-year Talent Visa,** decided within 30 days, open to:
     
     -   holders of a STEM PhD;
     -   graduates of the world's top 500 universities;
     -   people with a job offer of ₹30 lakh a year or more;
     -   founders of funded startups.
     
     The spouse may work. After 5 years the holder can apply for a new Permanent Residence Status, created by rules under the Immigration and Foreigners Act, 2025. OCI status is reserved by law for people of Indian origin, so a separate status is needed.  
     12\. **Visa-free entry for up to 90 days** for citizens of 50 countries, chosen for low overstay and security risk, notified by the Home Ministry under the same Act. Biometrics are taken on arrival.
     

**Every school of every faith**

13.  **One standard by one law.** A National School Standards Act, under the Concurrent List entry on education (List III, entry 25), applies to every institution that is a child's main school during school hours, for ages 6 to 18. Each teaches the national core curriculum (the daily block in item 1: maths, science and coding, plus languages) and sits the national tests in classes 3, 5 and 8 (item 4). It applies equally to government, private, madrasa, pathshala, gurukul, convent and seminary schools. It is a standards law only, separate from the RTE Act's admission quotas, so it fits within Article 30 as the courts read it.
14.  **Faith teaching as an extra.** Any school may teach religion, scripture or traditional knowledge outside core hours or at weekends. Article 28 already bars religious instruction in wholly state-funded schools; that stays.
15.  **Close the gap in the RTE Act.** Section 1(5) is deleted, so every child aged 6 to 14 has the same right to the same education wherever they study.
16.  **Minority rights stay whole.** Minority schools keep full control of admissions, management, staff, the language of instruction and faith teaching. Only the minimum core curriculum and testing are set.
17.  **Transition within 1 year.** Each institution either adopts the core curriculum or becomes a supplementary school, and its pupils join a nearby school for the school day. Teachers come from the same pool and training as item 6. Serving faith-school teachers can qualify to teach core subjects through a 6-month certified course.
18.  **Recognised qualifications.** Only board- or UGC-recognised qualifications count for jobs and further study, as the 2024 judgment holds.

## Cost and return

-   **AI tutor (estimate):** 24.7 crore students × ₹100 a year = ₹2,470 crore a year. ₹100 per active learner is the starting price; the final price is the median of three independent cost consultancies (How the government buys). Payment is per verified active learner, within 30 days. A tutor that fails an audit is de-certified within 7 days.
-   **Tablets (estimate):**
    -   About 6 crore government-school students in classes 3 to 8 share devices 4 to 1, which needs 1.5 crore tablets. At about ₹8,000 each, the one-time cost is ₹12,000 crore.
    -   The tablets are made in India to an open spec. Any certified maker supplies at the published price and is paid per verified delivery.
    -   India's yearly tablet market is several times smaller than this order (estimate), so delivery is phased: 50 lakh by month 6 and 1.5 crore by month 12. Rugged low-cost phones from the [Chapter 4](/the-third-freedom/digital-rails-for-every-citizen) line are also accepted.
    -   Late delivery costs 1% of order value per week.
-   **Census tests (estimate):** 3 grades × about 2.5 crore children × ₹50 = ₹375 crore a year.
-   **Maths specialists (estimate):** about 1.5 lakh cluster specialists × ₹6 lakh a year = ₹9,000 crore a year. Online teacher training is a small extra cost.
-   **Totals (estimate):**

| Item | Year 1 (₹ crore) | From year 2 (₹ crore a year) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| AI tutor | 2,470 | 2,470 |
| Tablets | 12,000 | 3,000 (replacement over 4 years) |
| Census tests | 375 | 375 |
| Maths specialists | 9,000 | 9,000 |
| Training | plus training |  |
| **Total** | **about 24,000** (₹2,470 + ₹12,000 + ₹375 + ₹9,000 crore, plus training) | **about 15,000** (₹2,470 + ₹375 + ₹9,000 + ₹3,000 crore) |

-   **Core-subject teachers in faith schools (estimate):** about 50,000 institutions needing core-subject teachers (basis: UP's roughly 25,000 madrasas plus faith schools elsewhere) × 2 teachers × ₹4 lakh a year = **₹4,000 crore a year**, on top of the totals above. The AI tutor and national digital textbooks add no cost per school.
-   **Year 1** comes from the net subsidy saving in [Chapter 23](/the-third-freedom/a-hand-up-and-an-equal-chance): after this ₹24,000 crore and a ₹10,000 crore welfare reserve, about ₹2.26 lakh crore remains.
-   **From year 2** it is a standing line in the education budget, paid from the larger steady-state saving in [Chapter 23](/the-third-freedom/a-hand-up-and-an-equal-chance).
-   **Return from students who stay (estimate):** if 10% of the 18 lakh students abroad study in India, 1.8 lakh students × about ₹20 lakh each (an assumed total cost of a degree abroad) = ₹36,000 crore kept in India.
-   **Return from tourism (estimate):** 20 lakh extra visitors × about ₹1.2 lakh spent each = ₹24,000 crore a year in foreign exchange, plus hotel and transport jobs.
-   **Return from one core in every school:** lakhs of children gain the maths and science that open skilled jobs and raise lifetime earnings, and faith schools keep their pupils and their mission.

## Timeline

**First 30 days:**

-   The daily STEM timetable is notified, and NCERT releases scripted lessons.
-   The AI-tutor spec, certification test and API standard are published.
-   The RTE Act amendment (including the deletion of Section 1(5)), the National School Standards Bill and the UGC Act Section 22 amendment are tabled (by ordinance if Parliament is not sitting).
-   The core curriculum standard is published, and every institution that is a child's main school is listed, using the UDISE+ school census and state madrasa and pathshala boards.
-   The university disclosure portal goes live.
-   The Talent Visa and the visa-free country list are notified.

**Within 1 year:**

-   The AI tutor is live in all 22 languages. 1.5 crore tablets are delivered.
-   The first census tests are held by month 6, with results public by month 7.
-   Bottom-10% schools run their 90-day plans. Those that fail the re-test move to new management by month 12.
-   The first audited university outcome tables are published.
-   Every listed institution teaches the full core curriculum, or its pupils are placed in a nearby school, and every child in classes 3, 5 and 8 sits the national tests.

**Outcomes after year 1:**

-   The second census test (month 18) shows a gain of at least 5 points in class 3 maths.
-   Class 3 subtraction rises from 33.7% to 50% within 3 years.

## Guardrails

-   **Tutor accuracy:** at least 90% on a 10,000-question NCERT bank in each language before launch, audited every quarter. Below 85% means suspension in that language until it is fixed.
-   **Child data:** under the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023: no ads and no sale of data. A breach of the children's-data duties costs up to ₹200 crore, and a failure of security safeguards up to ₹250 crore, the Act's maximum.
-   **Test integrity:** randomised questions on tablets and external invigilators. A school caught cheating is treated as bottom-10% at once.
-   **False university outcomes:** the institution is closed, its director is banned for life, and all fees are refunded to students.
-   **Visa-free list:** if a country's overstay rate goes above 1%, its visa-free entry is suspended within 30 days.
-   **Talent Visa:** a 30-day background check, and biometrics at entry.
-   **Same rule for every faith:** the inspection list and results are published every quarter by school type and faith, so no group is singled out.
-   **A place before any move:** no pupil moves until a seat is confirmed within 1 km for classes 1 to 5 and 3 km for classes 6 to 8, the RTE neighbourhood norms. Hostels and meals stay where they are.
-   **Results in minority schools:** a school in the bottom 10% on national tests for 2 years gets new management (item 5). A minority school keeps its management and gets a state-paid academic mentor for 2 years; if it is still in the bottom 10% after that, it loses recognition as a main school and its pupils are placed elsewhere.
-   **Core-subject teachers:** every core-subject teacher passes the certification test within 12 months of appointment.

## Limits to know

Learning gains need 3 to 5 years of daily practice, so the turnaround is an outcome after year 1; every policy step is in place within the year. Article 21A makes schooling free for every child aged 6 to 14, so government-school fees stay zero.

Closing schools because of their religion, or taking over the management of a minority school, would breach Articles 25 to 30 and is not proposed. The 2014 ruling keeps minority schools outside the RTE Act until a larger bench decides otherwise, so the plan relies on a separate standards-only law, which the courts have consistently allowed.
