## The plan

1.  **Defence Startup Mission.** It is run by a CEO from industry who holds cabinet-minister rank on a fixed 1-year mission term with published targets, reports to the Prime Minister (the [Chapter 12](/the-third-freedom/a-lean-state-that-delivers) mission rule) and works with the Defence Minister.
2.  **Guaranteed buyer.** Any Indian product that passes a published field-trial standard gets an order within 30 days: at least 3 times the prototype value or ₹25 crore, whichever is higher, with a repeat option of up to 50% more. Each trial standard publishes a fixed price per unit before the window opens, set as the median of 3 independent cost consultancies' estimates (How the government buys) and never above the latest price paid for an imported equivalent. No price talks follow the trial. Every product that passes is certified, and any unit in India can order it at that price, paid per verified delivery. If 2 or more products pass, the guaranteed first order is shared equally.
3.  **Trials on a clock.** Standing trial windows every quarter at named ranges (desert, high altitude, coastal). Specs are frozen when a window opens; a changed requirement starts the next window and does not cancel the current one. Results come in 45 days for small systems (drones, counter-drone, sensors) and 90 days for complex systems, scored against published pass marks.
4.  **Paid in 30 days.** Payment is due 30 days after acceptance. Acceptance is deemed 21 days after delivery unless the user unit files a written defect report on the portal, which the trial panel rules on within 14 days. Late payment carries interest of 1.5% a month, paid automatically from the budget of the unit that caused the delay.
5.  **Earmark.** 5% of defence capital spending, about ₹10,965 crore (₹2,19,306 crore × 5%), is ring-fenced for products that passed a field trial in the last 12 months, from any Indian firm of any size or age. It comes only from the uncommitted (new-contract) share of capital acquisition, so payments on existing contracts are never delayed, and the Ministry of Defence publishes the committed and uncommitted split within 30 days. At least 50% goes to the priority areas below, checked every quarter. Unspent money returns to the general capital pool at year end.
6.  **Spiral contracts.** Drones and electronic-warfare kit are bought on a supply, service and upgrade model with software updates every quarter, so fielded kit keeps pace with 6 to 8 week jammer cycles.
7.  **Ownership.** Classified work is done only in a 100% Indian-owned and controlled unit; a ring-fenced subsidiary is fine (Sovereignty first, Tier 1). Non-classified work follows [Chapter 10](/the-third-freedom/built-by-indians-open-to-the-world)'s one rule: in unlisted firms below ₹500 crore of project capital spending, foreign investors hold up to 50% of the economics and Indians 51% of the votes; listed firms and ₹500 crore+ projects follow the defence FDI rule (74% automatic, more by approval).
8.  **Priority areas.** Autonomous and swarm drones, counter-drone systems, AI border surveillance (radar, cameras and sensor fusion), electronic warfare, loitering munitions and secure communications.
9.  **The legal route.** The Defence Acquisition Council adopts these rules as amendments to the Defence Acquisition Procedure (capital buying) and the Defence Procurement Manual 2025 (revenue buying). No new Act is needed.

## Cost and return

-   **Who pays.** The existing capital budget. No new money is needed, only a ring-fence.
-   **Market.** ₹10,965 crore a year of guaranteed demand is about 4.7 times everything executed through iDEX so far (₹10,965 crore ÷ ₹2,326 crore). That can support about 200 firms at ₹50 crore of revenue each (estimate).
-   **Savings.** The published price never exceeds the latest import price, and every order placed in India keeps the money and the know-how at home.
-   **Technology.** Indian flight controllers and autopilots, NavIC (India's satellite navigation), Indian radio links and AI vision models trained on Indian terrain. Trial data goes into a secure national dataset that any cleared firm can use.
-   **Who builds.** Startups and other new suppliers compete as prime contractors on the same trial rules as everyone. DPSUs (defence public-sector firms) and large private firms act as integrators, with a target of 20% of their contract value bought from trial-passed new suppliers. DRDO labs license designs at a 2% royalty.
-   **Jobs.** At about ₹50 lakh of revenue per engineering job, ₹10,965 crore supports about 22,000 direct high-skill jobs (estimate: ₹10,965 crore ÷ ₹0.5 crore).
-   **Exports.** Trial-proven kit gets an export authorisation to friendly nations within 30 days, building on FY26's ₹38,424 crore.

## Timeline

**First 30 days:**

-   The Defence Acquisition Council approves the Mission, the 5% earmark, the fixed-price rule and the 30-day payment rule.
-   Trial standards, pass marks and prices are published for the first 10 priority products.
-   The Mission CEO is named, and one online portal opens for trial booking.
-   The first quarterly trial window is announced, to be held by day 60.

**Within 1 year:**

-   4 trial windows held; at least 100 products trialled; at least 40 orders placed within 30 days of passing, counting windows 1 to 3 (orders from window 4 may land in month 13).
-   At least ₹8,000 crore of the earmark committed (73%).
-   Average payment time of 30 days or less, audited by the Controller General of Defence Accounts.
-   Drones bought for the forces reach at least 60% Indian content by value.

**Outcomes after year 1:**

-   Indian-made high-end cameras, thermal sensors and small jet engines replace the items on the 12-month exemption list.

## Guardrails

-   **Trusted parts only.** No flight controller, radio, camera or firmware from a source that fails the trusted-source test (Sovereignty first) in any system bought. An accredited lab audits the bill of materials. A breach cancels the order and bars the firm for 5 years; fraud on origin means a lifetime ban and prosecution.
-   **12-month exemption list.** High-end cameras, thermal sensors and small engines with no Indian supplier may come from trusted sources for 12 months only. An item leaves the list as soon as an Indian substitute passes trials.
-   **Trial integrity.** Each trial panel has 1 user officer, 1 DRDO scientist and 1 independent expert. Scores are published within 7 days.
-   **Bond and failure.** Every order is backed by a bond of 10% of its value (20% for orders of ₹100 crore or more), posted as a bank guarantee or insurance surety bond. Each in-service failure within 12 months forfeits 20% of that bond.
-   **Human control.** Every use of lethal force by an autonomous system needs a human decision, and every engagement is logged.
-   **No share cap.** A firm may win any share of the earmark. Orders follow field-proven results, and the guaranteed first order keeps new firms coming in.

## Limits to know

High-end drone cameras, thermal sensors and small jet engines cannot be fully made in India within 1 year. The 12-month exemption list from trusted sources bridges the gap, while a separate challenge funds Indian substitutes, which are an outcome after year 1.
