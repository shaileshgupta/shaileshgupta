## The plan

1.  **Government fleet.** From day 30, ministries, PSUs, police and state governments buy or lease only EVs. Old vehicles are replaced at end of life or within 12 months, whichever comes first.
2.  **Buses.** Every new city and state bus is electric, run on per-km contracts: a private operator owns and runs the bus, and the state pays per verified km. Any operator that passes the open e-bus spec and certification test can serve any city at the published rate per km (Aadhaar playbook; How the government buys). Routes go first come first served within district quotas; payment within 30 days; de-certification within 7 days on a failed audit. Target: **50,000 e-buses ordered in 12 months**.
3.  **Taxis and three-wheelers.** From month 6, only EVs can be newly registered as taxis, ride-hailing cars or auto-rickshaws. This is a rule of the vehicle register and the one Business ID, not a permit (No licence raj).
4.  **New private sales: a zero-emission vehicle (ZEV) quota.** Each maker sells a rising share of EVs or buys credits from makers who beat the quota. Two-wheelers step up 25%, 50%, 75% and 100% by quarter. Cars and trucks rise 10 points a quarter. Motors that use no imported rare-earth magnets count double.
5.  **Retrofit loans.** Any owner converting a petrol two-wheeler or auto under 8 years old with a certified kit can get a bank loan at market rate with a government guarantee, repaid from fuel savings. No interest subsidy.
6.  **Highway chargers.** One fast-charging site on each side of every national highway every 25 km. Each site goes to the first certified operator that posts the bond, at a site fee set by the national rate card (How the government buys). Sites are packaged one busy with three quiet, so no top-up is needed.
7.  **Nuclear 100x.** A Nuclear 100x Act sets the target in law: from 8.78 GW to about **880 GW**.
    -   **Standard fleet.** Only 3 certified designs are built: the 700 MW PHWR, the 220 MW Bharat Small Reactor and one certified large light-water design. A design is approved once; each repeat unit needs only a 90-day site review.
    -   **Orders.** 100 GW is ordered within 1 year. Any certified consortium builds at a published price per MW, posts the bond upfront and is paid per inspected milestone. The company that owns and runs each reactor is 100% Indian (Sovereignty first, Tier 1); foreign firms join as suppliers and licensors of designs and parts. Startups enter through a small and micro reactor track with a national test site and a guaranteed first order once AERB certifies a design. There is no turnover or experience threshold.
    -   **Sites.** About 300 sites are reserved in year 1, starting with retiring coal plants, which already have land, water and grid lines.
    -   **Supply chain.** Forging, pressure-vessel and steam-generator plants get ready plots and same-day registration, to reach 40 GW a year of component output.
    -   **Fuel.** A 2-year uranium stockpile, long-term contracts, overseas mine stakes held by NPCIL and KABIL (Khanij Bidesh India Limited) on commercial terms, and fast-breeder and thorium reactors ordered once the Kalpakkam fast breeder runs at full power.
    -   **People and safety.** AERB becomes an independent statutory regulator with 10 times its staff. 50 nuclear training centres train 1 lakh technicians and engineers a year. A deep geological waste repository site is chosen in year 1.
8.  **Open power market.** Pass the Electricity (Amendment) Bill so several discoms can sell over one shared wire network. States that privatise or franchise loss-making discoms get extra borrowing room of 0.5% of their GSDP.

## Cost and return

-   **Oil saved (estimate):** road fuel ≈ 40 Mt petrol + 70% of 91.4 Mt diesel ≈ 104 Mt. Every 10% replaced saves 10.4 Mt ≈ 76 million barrels ≈ **₹53,900 crore ($5.7 billion) a year** at $75 (₹7,090) a barrel.
-   **Buses and highway chargers (estimate):**

| Build | Arithmetic | Capital (₹ crore) | Who pays |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Buses | 50,000 × ₹1.2 crore | ≈ **60,000** | raised by private operators; the state pays per km, backed by a central payment-security fund |
| Highway chargers | about 1.46 lakh km ÷ 25 km × 2 sides ≈ **11,700 sites** × ₹40 lakh (two 60 kW chargers plus grid link) | ≈ **4,700** | private money earned back from charging fees |
| **Total** | 60,000 + 4,700 | about **64,700** | mostly private |

-   **Charger grid lines:** the existing ₹2,000 crore PM E-DRIVE charger fund pays only for the public grid line up to each site, which stays part of the shared network and open to every operator; the chargers themselves get no subsidy.
-   **Batteries (estimate):** two-wheeler sales ≈ 2.2 crore a year (25.5 lakh EVs × 58% two-wheelers ÷ 6.6%). All electric × 3 kWh ≈ **66 GWh** of cells, ≈ **₹59,000 crore** at ₹9,000 a kWh, imported at the single 5% duty at first. A 3 kWh pack (about ₹27,000) replaces about 250 litres of petrol a year, about ₹9,600 of crude, so it repays its import cost in about 3 years. Cell factories on ready plots cut imports from year 2.
-   **Nuclear (estimate):** 880 GW × ₹12 crore per MW (fleet price; today's 700 MW PHWR fleet costs about ₹15 crore per MW) ≈ **₹105 lakh crore ($1.1 trillion) over about 25 years**, about ₹4 to 5 lakh crore a year. Year 1: 100 GW of contracts ≈ ₹12 lakh crore, with about 10% (₹1.2 lakh crore) spent on design, sites and long-lead forgings. Private consortia finance it against 40-year tariffs set by CERC. The budget pays only the existing ₹20,000 crore Nuclear Energy Mission for design work.
-   **Nuclear output (estimate):** 880 GW × 85% × 8,760 hours ≈ 6,500 TWh a year, about 3.5 times India's power use today: enough to replace all coal power and run EVs, green hydrogen, desalination and data centres.
-   **Fuel-tax effect (estimate):** about 3% of road fuel is replaced in year 1, reducing the central part of the [Chapter 1](/the-third-freedom/tax-keep-what-you-earn) fuel levy by about ₹10,000 crore (3% × ₹3.3 lakh crore) and the states' part by about ₹9,000 crore (3% × ₹3 lakh crore). The central fall rises by ₹25,000 to 35,000 crore a year after that. Both are covered by the [Chapter 20](/the-third-freedom/built-right-built-to-last) overrun savings (about ₹57,000 crore a year), and [Chapter 1](/the-third-freedom/tax-keep-what-you-earn)'s levy forecast falls by the measured amount each year.

## Timeline

**First 30 days:**

-   Notify the EV-only rule for central ministries and PSUs; publish the government fleet count by ministry, age and fuel.
-   Publish the e-bus spec, certification test and per-km rate; states order the first 25,000 e-buses from certified operators. Publish the draft ZEV quota rules.
-   Publish the 11,700 highway charger sites on a map and open them to certified operators.
-   Introduce the Nuclear 100x Act and the AERB independence bill. Issue the call for the first 100 GW across the 3 designs at a published price per MW.

**Within 1 year:**

-   50,000 e-buses ordered and 20,000 running.
-   All new taxi, ride-hailing and three-wheeler registrations electric.
-   Two-wheeler ZEV quota at 100% (subject to the supply brake); car quota at about 40%.
-   11,700 highway sites live at 97% uptime.
-   Nuclear: Act passed; 100 GW of contracts signed; 300 sites reserved; first concrete poured at the 10 reactor sites (7 GW) already in pre-project work; site consents for the first coal-plant sites.
-   Electricity (Amendment) Act passed; at least 5 states with private or franchised discoms.

**Outcomes after year 1:** small reactors take 5 to 7 years from first concrete to power, so new nuclear electricity flows from about year 6. At 40 GW a year after that, 880 GW is reached in about 25 years. Cars and trucks reach 100% electric after year 1 as the quota keeps rising 10 points a quarter.

## Guardrails

-   **Chargers:** below 97% uptime in a month costs ₹10,000 per site per day. Below 90% for 3 months, the site passes to the next certified operator. Public chargers buy at least 50% renewable power by month 12.
-   **ZEV quota:** ₹50,000 per missing car and ₹5,000 per missing two-wheeler, doubling each quarter a maker stays short, paid into the charging fund; 50% is refunded for each EV sold above quota in the next quarter. Credits count on registration date, so a pre-quota rush costs credits too.
-   **Supply brake:** if national electric two-wheeler output at month 9 is below 70% of monthly demand, the month-12 step holds at 75% for one quarter, then resumes.
-   **Bus payments:** operators are paid within 30 days from the payment-security fund; a state that pays late loses the same amount from its central transfers.
-   **Retrofit guarantee:** capped at ₹5,000 crore; EMIs equal 70% of the measured fuel saving.
-   **Reactors:** no deadline on safety. AERB rules on a new design within 180 days and on each repeat unit within 90 days, publishing every ruling within 7 days. A consortium 90 days late on a milestone loses its bond and the unit passes to the next certified builder. Operators carry insurance up to the SHANTI Act liability cap, backed by a government pool; every safety report is public.
-   **Discoms:** one cost-based tariff for every consumer within 12 months, in 4 equal quarterly steps, with each new bill shown 30 days ahead. Households under the [Chapter 23](/the-third-freedom/a-hand-up-and-an-equal-chance) income line get the [Chapter 23](/the-third-freedom/a-hand-up-and-an-equal-chance) transfer. Private discoms cut AT&C loss by 5 points in 12 months, or their supply area passes to the next certified operator, as with every network service ([Chapter 12](/the-third-freedom/a-lean-state-that-delivers)).
-   **Battery safety:** all packs pass AIS-156 testing; any recall is reported within 48 hours.
-   **European bar for vehicles:** every new vehicle type meets the EU Euro 7 standard (Regulation (EU) 2024/1257) within 1 year, including its brake-particle limits and minimum battery-durability rule for electric cars. Where Indian rules still trail Euro 7, the gap is published every year with the date it closes.

## Limits to know

No new nuclear electricity can flow within 1 year, and fuel is the hard limit: 880 GW of today's reactors would need about 1.4 lakh tonnes of uranium a year, more than twice world mine output (60,213 tonnes in 2024). The first third can run on bought and stockpiled uranium; the rest depends on fast-breeder and thorium reactors. Discom privatisation needs each state's consent because electricity distribution is a state matter; the Centre offers states extra borrowing room to do it (item 8).
