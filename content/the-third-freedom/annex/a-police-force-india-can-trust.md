## The plan

1.  **National Police Standards Act.** One rulebook for fitness, training, promotion and equipment, which states adopt through a Model Police Act. 20% of police-modernisation and Finance Commission police grants go only to adopting states (Article 282). [Chapter 28](/the-third-freedom/locking-in-the-third-freedom) moves police to the Concurrent List; until it commences, willing states can ask Parliament to legislate for them under Article 252. States keep command of their forces.
2.  **Army culture through people and training, not soldiers on the street.**
    -   One open merit exam for every new constable and sub-inspector post, with army-standard fitness, drill and discipline tests. From the day the [Chapter 28](/the-third-freedom/locking-in-the-third-freedom) equal-opportunity amendment commences, there is no quota for any group, and the existing ex-Agniveer quotas in the central forces fold into this one rule; until then each state's present law applies to the same exam. Ex-servicemen and ex-Agniveers compete on the same test, where their training counts on merit.
    -   Up to 20% of new Deputy SP posts are open to lateral entry for anyone with 5 or more years of leadership in a disciplined service or a large organisation (a retired Major qualifies), who passes the same exam and a 6-month law course.
    -   Every recruit does a 9-month academy course with military-style drill and 6 months of law and human-rights training within it.
3.  **Fitness and integrity every year.** An annual age-graded fitness test to the central armed police standard; two failures in a row mean desk duty and no promotion. Every officer's net worth is compiled automatically each year in the asset register ([Chapter 13](/the-third-freedom/clean-money-trusted-public-life)).
4.  **Body cameras and working CCTV.** Every frontline officer wears a body camera on duty. All station CCTV streams to a state cloud with 18-month retention. Turning a camera off without a logged reason is misconduct, and a pattern of it means dismissal after a hearing. Cameras and cloud follow the Aadhaar playbook: an open spec with hash-stamped, tamper-evident video, a certification test, and any certified Indian maker, startups included, can supply any force at the published price, paid per verified delivery within 30 days (How the government buys).
5.  **Independent complaints body.** A statutory Police Complaints Authority in every state and district, chaired by a retired judge, with its own investigators. Complaints of death, torture, rape or illegal detention are decided within 90 days, and findings bind the department.
6.  **Fill every vacancy.** Rolling recruitment through one national common exam, results within 30 days. Unfilled posts stay vacant rather than lowering the cut-off, and probation lasts 2 years. Extra academy seats come from central armed police and army training centres and from leased university campuses at a published price.
7.  **Contracts and market pay.** Every police post, constable to DGP, becomes a 5-year renewable contract with NPS pension ([Chapter 12](/the-third-freedom/a-lean-state-that-delivers)). Serving officers can convert with 6 months' pay as a bonus within 1 year, or keep Article 311 protection until the [Chapter 28](/the-third-freedom/locking-in-the-third-freedom) amendment commences. Pay is the private-sector rate for the same skill and responsibility, set by the [Chapter 12](/the-third-freedom/a-lean-state-that-delivers) Pay Benchmark Board, plus a 15% risk premium for frontline duty; 20% of pay is a bonus tied to the published score. Nobody's pay is cut. A proven bribe means dismissal, forfeiture of 5 years' pay and any unpaid bonus, a lifetime ban from government posts, and prosecution. Intelligence agency staff stay permanent.
8.  **National Internal Intelligence Act.** It gives the Intelligence Bureau a statutory basis for the first time. Surveillance and searches need a warrant from a designated High Court judge, decided within 24 hours. The agency gathers intelligence only; it has no power to arrest or punish. It reports to a Parliamentary Intelligence Committee with equal ruling and opposition seats and an opposition chair, meeting in closed session, with an annual public audit.
9.  **Sabotage special courts.** One per state, using the special courts that already exist under the NIA Act and UAPA, with added judges. They try terrorism, espionage, terror financing and physical attacks on critical infrastructure such as the grid or pipelines. The trial ends within 90 days of a full charge-sheet. The accused gets state-paid senior counsel and an automatic High Court appeal decided within 90 days. The maximum lawful punishment applies: death only where existing law allows it, after full trial and High Court confirmation; otherwise life without remission, plus confiscation of assets.

## Cost and return

-   **State vacancies:** 5.31 lakh posts × ₹6 lakh a year all-in = **₹31,900 crore a year** (estimate), paid by states for posts they have already sanctioned.
-   **Central vacancies:** 93,139 posts × ₹6 lakh = about ₹5,600 crore a year (estimate), within the Home Ministry's sanctioned strength.
-   **State police spending today:** ₹1,275 per person × 145 crore people ≈ ₹1.85 lakh crore a year. Filling state vacancies adds about 17%.
-   **Market pay for states (estimate):** salaries are about 75% of spending, ≈ ₹1.39 lakh crore. Constables already earn at or above the private market, so their present pay is their floor.

| Pay item (estimate) | Arithmetic | Cost (₹ crore a year) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Risk premium | 15% × (₹1.39 lakh crore + ₹31,900 crore) | ≈ 25,600 |
| Officer raise | Inspector to DGP ≈ 12% of the pay bill (≈ ₹16,700 crore), raised 1.75 times on average | ≈ +12,500 |
| Pay total | ₹25,600 + ₹12,500 | ≈ 38,100 |

-   **Total extra for states:** ₹31,900 + ₹38,100 ≈ **₹70,000 crore a year**, about 0.2% of GDP (estimate). It is funded inside the [Chapter 12](/the-third-freedom/a-lean-state-that-delivers) rule that caps each state's wage bill at today's level in real terms: police is the one cadre that grows, paid for by closing or selling state boards, corporations and departments the market can run. Pay and hiring phase in over the year as contracts are signed.
-   **One-time equipment (estimate):** the Centre pays 60% (≈ ₹2,730 crore) for adopting states from existing police-modernisation grants. Cloud storage ≈ ₹600 crore a year.

| Equipment (estimate) | Arithmetic | One-time cost (₹ crore) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Body cameras | 10 lakh × ₹20,000 | 2,000 |
| Station CCTV upgrade | 17,000 stations × ₹15 lakh | 2,550 |
| Total | ₹2,000 + ₹2,550 | 4,550 |

-   **Complaints authorities:** 36 states and UTs × ₹25 crore = ₹900 crore a year (estimate).
-   **Special courts:** 36 courts × 3 judges × ₹2 crore all-in = ₹216 crore a year (estimate).
-   **Return:** faster trials cut the cost of holding undertrials, who are **74%** of all prisoners, and safer streets raise investment. No new central money is needed, so nothing is added to the [Chapter 1](/the-third-freedom/tax-keep-what-you-earn) balance.
-   **Jobs:** about 6.2 lakh police jobs plus about 5,000 technology jobs (estimate).

## Timeline

**First 30 days:**

-   The Model Police Act and the National Internal Intelligence Bill are drafted and tabled. The Bill also amends UAPA Section 43D for the 90-day trial rule.
-   The open merit exam and the army-standard fitness test are notified and offered to states with the grant link.
-   The body-camera spec and certification test are published; certified makers can supply at once.
-   Every station CCTV is working within 30 days, with each district SP personally answerable to the state DGP.
-   Existing NIA and UAPA special courts are designated as sabotage courts, with extra judges and the 90-day rule.

**Within 1 year:**

-   All vacancies advertised, at least 80% appointed, and at least 40% passed out and on duty (first batch in academy by month 3).
-   All frontline officers on body cameras.
-   Complaints authorities working in every state.
-   The Intelligence Act passed and the parliamentary committee sitting.
-   The first Agniveer batch, about 24,400 recruits, finishes its four years from late 2026, and the about 18,300 (75%) not retained can sit the open exam.

**Outcomes after year 1:**

-   The remaining recruits pass out by month 21.

## Guardrails

-   "Sabotage" is defined in law as violence, espionage, terror financing or physical attack on critical infrastructure. **Protest, criticism, journalism, strikes and opposition politics are expressly excluded**, and any case built on them is quashed with costs against the officer.
-   Every warrant is decided within 24 hours, logged, and counted in the annual public audit.
-   Sabotage trials end within 90 days of the charge-sheet; if the prosecution misses this, the accused gets bail as of right. A High Court may extend the deadline once, by 60 days, with written reasons published in redacted form.
-   Custodial death or torture: a magistrate inquiry within 48 hours, suspension of the station head, and the complaints body's decision within 90 days.
-   A body camera off without a logged reason during an arrest: the arrest is presumed unlawful unless the police prove otherwise.
-   Footage is encrypted and opened only by court order or a complaints body request ([Chapter 4](/the-third-freedom/digital-rails-for-every-citizen) privacy ledger). Leaking it is a crime with up to 3 years in jail.
-   A quarterly public dashboard per state shows vacancies, conviction rate, complaints received and closed, and custodial deaths. Police contracts are renewed only on published scores for fitness, conviction rate and complaints upheld.

## Limits to know

Moving police to the Concurrent List needs a constitutional amendment ratified by at least half the states; [Chapter 28](/the-third-freedom/locking-in-the-third-freedom) sends it to the states within the first 30 days (target 15 states by day 60), and until then national standards travel through grants and Article 252. Punishment without trial is not lawful under Article 21 and is not part of this plan; the hard line is fast trials and the maximum lawful sentence.
